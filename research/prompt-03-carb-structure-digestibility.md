## Prior-claim bullets (mapped to citation numbers)

**A. "Slow-digesting" is a host-enzyme label, and it does not define colonic substrate availability.**

- The RDS/SDS/RS classification is operationally defined by controlled enzymic hydrolysis with pancreatin and amyloglucosidase; RDS and SDS reflect the *rate* of small-intestinal digestion, and only the RS value is offered as "a guide to the amounts of starch likely to enter the colon for fermentation." [1]
- SDS is framed throughout the primary review literature as a *glycemic* construct concerning "the rate and extent of digestion and absorption in the small intestine," with a proposed extended-glycemic-index readout — i.e. a host endpoint, not a microbial one. [2]
- Therefore, in an in vivo dietary system the host acts as a filter: what reaches the colon is the *undigested residue*, whose amount and chemistry differ from the ingested formulation. In an ex vivo fecal fermentation the substrate is applied directly and that filter is absent or, at most, approximated by a pre-digestion step. [1, 2, 21]
- The standard consensus pre-digestion protocol (INFOGEST 2.0) uses fixed meal:fluid ratios and a constant pH per phase and is stated by its own authors to be **unsuitable for simulating digestion kinetics** — the very property that distinguishes a slow- from a rapid-digesting formulation. [23]
- In vitro colon-model methodologists state directly that "what happens in the upper digestion tract and absorption prior to colon is also of crucial importance" to whether a colon model is representative. [21]

**B. Carbohydrate structure and physical form — not the digestibility label — drive which microbes respond.**

- Fibre/starch chemistry resolves into thousands of "discrete structures" that align with distinct bacterial gene clusters; substrate identity is therefore a selective, not a bulk-nutritional, variable. [4]
- Fermentation of granular starches is governed **predominantly by granule surface features rather than molecular and supra-molecular structure**, with A-type polymorphs fermenting markedly faster than B-type. [6]
- Holding chemistry constant and changing only physical accessibility (alginate-entrapped starch microspheres vs RS2) produced 2.4× higher mol% butyrate and a phylum-level Bacteroidetes→Firmicutes shift that RS2 did not produce. [7]
- Reasons starch escapes digestion include "protection provided by other polymers, particle structure, retrogradation, or chemical cross-linking" — i.e. processing history is a first-order determinant. [16, 3, 8]
- Utilization also requires the right degrader to be present: RS3 fermentation was poor in donors lacking *Ruminococcus bromii*, and adding *R. bromii* to such a donor's fecal community greatly increased RS3 fermentation in vitro. [15, 16]

**C. SCFA responses are analyte-specific; uniform "more SCFA" is not the expected pattern.**

- Across eight native starches spanning very high to low RS, **total SCFA production was similar between ingredients, while acetate and butyrate molar ratios differed** (0.48–0.56 and 0.17–0.25 respectively), as did per-analyte kinetics — Rmax differed for propionate and butyrate but *not* for acetate. [9]
- In a human dose-response RCT, two RS4 chemistries directed output toward *either* propionate *or* butyrate; effects were consistent within treatment groups and **plateaued at 35 g/day**, i.e. dose-saturating rather than linear. [5]
- Propionate and butyrate are produced by different pathways in different taxa; hexose-derived propionate is dominated by the succinate route (Bacteroidetes, Negativicutes) while butyrate is concentrated in clostridial clusters IV/XIVa. [11, 12, 18]
- Acetate and butyrate are metabolically coupled rather than independent: net butyrate formation involves acetate uptake, and % butyrate rises as total SCFA rises and pH falls. [14, 11]
- Cross-feeding of lactate, succinate and 1,2-propanediol between taxa is a required intermediate step, so a given analyte's accumulation reflects a *network*, not a single substrate→product conversion. [11, 17]
- In an ex vivo two-donor bread fermentation, acetate and propionate converged between donors by 24 h while **butyrate remained donor-divergent** — analyte- and timepoint-specific behaviour in a design close to ours. [25]

**D. "More substrate → more SCFA" is not a safe universal rule.**

- Metabolic output depends "not only on available substrate, but also on the gut environment, with pH playing a major role." [18, 17]
- pH is not a fixed background condition in a batch system: it falls as acids accumulate, and mildly acidic pH suppresses *Bacteroides* while favouring *E. rectale*-related butyrate producers, restructuring the community mid-incubation. [13, 14]
- The **background medium is itself a substrate**. Systematic optimization work shows medium nutrient concentration and starting cell density determine community drift and Enterobacteriaceae overgrowth, and that a *less* nutrient-dense medium **increases the measurable impact of an added fibre**. A rich basal medium can therefore mask a real substrate effect. [22]
- Utilization is gated by the presence of appropriate degraders and cross-feeders, so an unutilized substrate yields no SCFA regardless of dose. [15, 11]
- Dose-response is saturating, not linear, at nutritionally realistic intakes. [5]
- Fibre-blend effects are neither additive nor phenotype-independent: INU+RS and BG+RS produced different fermentation and metabolic signatures in the same trial programme. [10]

**E. Model system determines what can be observed.**

- Batch, semi-continuous and multi-compartment continuous systems (SHIME/M-SHIME, TIM/TIM-2, ARCOL, MBRA, Copenhagen MiniGut, DGS) differ in retention time, pH control, compartmentalisation and mucosal phase. [19, 20]
- The explicit design trade-off is between a complex biomimetic system aiming for in vivo comparability and a simple system that may lack physiological realism but is "sufficient to understand the fate of some specific components." [21]
- Continuous multi-compartment models let substrate source alter ecological network topology and per-analyte cumulative production over days; batch systems observe a single closed accumulation curve. [24, 19]
- Age-matched model configuration matters: a toddler-configured colon model reproduced **higher % acetate and lower % propionate and butyrate** than the adult configuration, consistent with in vivo paediatric data. [26]
- Donor identity is a major variance component in ex vivo fermentation, independent of substrate. [25, 22]

## Conflicts / nuance

1. **In vivo anchor vs ex vivo design — different systems, different questions.** Plaza-Díaz et al. [27] observed higher fecal *acetate and propionate* under slow-digestion carbohydrate replacement in HFD rats over 4 weeks. That is an in vivo, host-filtered, chronic-adaptation result in a rodent. Our design removes the host filter, uses human adolescent donors, and measures 0→48 h net accumulation. Convergence would be interesting; divergence would be expected and unremarkable. The anchor should motivate the question, never validate our answer.

2. **Total SCFA vs SCFA profile point in opposite directions.** Giuberti et al. [9] found total SCFA *similar* across starches while molar ratios differed; Deehan et al. [5] found structure-directed *individual* analyte shifts. If a study is powered on total SCFA it may miss the real effect; if powered per-analyte it may find effects that do not sum to a total change. State which we tested.

3. **pH is both a covariate and a mediator.** Duncan [13] and LaBouyer [14] together imply that in a batch system SCFA accumulation lowers pH, which then restructures the community and shifts butyrate stoichiometry. In an uncontrolled-pH batch design, "carbohydrate effect on butyrate" and "acid-accumulation effect on butyrate" are partially confounded. This is a genuine limitation to disclose, not a finding to explain away.

4. **Background medium can create or erase a substrate effect.** Poppe et al. [22] show a nutrient-rich basal medium compresses the measurable fibre signal. Any null carbohydrate effect must be interpreted against medium richness, not asserted as biological absence.

5. **Donor variance may exceed substrate variance, and is analyte-specific.** Pirkola [25] found donor-driven butyrate divergence persisting at 24 h while acetate/propionate converged. Gerasimidis [28] found substrate-driven SCFA differences with no group difference between IBD and healthy donors. Both caution against reading a group × carbohydrate interaction too strongly.

6. **Physical form can outweigh chemical class.** Kaur [7] and Wang [6] both find that accessibility/surface properties dominate. A formulation labelled by digestibility class may behave in the colon like a different class entirely depending on particle size, entrapment, and processing.

7. **Adult-derived expectations may not hold for adolescents.** Fournier [26] shows a paediatric colonic configuration systematically favours acetate over propionate and butyrate. Most of Tier B was established in adult systems.

8. ⚠ **Gerasimidis [28] is a claim-lock hazard.** Its stated conclusion — comparable fermentative capacity despite dysbiosis — structurally resembles an "obesity equivalence / preserved fermentation capacity" claim. Cite it as a *design precedent* (donor-group × substrate batch culture) only.

## What we must not overclaim (given claim lock)

- **No SDC superiority.** None of these citations support a claim that a slow-digesting formulation produces more acetate, propionate or butyrate than a rapid-digesting one. [5] and [9] support *divergent profiles by structure*, which is a different and weaker claim. [27] is a rodent in vivo result and cannot be transferred.
- **No obesity-group equivalence or "preserved fermentation capacity."** Do not repurpose [28] for this. Non-significance in our design is a null, not equivalence; no equivalence testing is cited or performed.
- **No *Fusicatenibacter* claims.** Nothing in this corpus identifies *Fusicatenibacter* as an SDC utilizer or a butyrate producer. The keystone-degrader literature retrieved here concerns *R. bromii* [15, 16] and the butyrate literature concerns *Roseburia* / *E. rectale* / clostridial clusters IV and XIVa [13, 14, 18]. Do not substitute one taxon for another.
- **No absolute-abundance claims from relative data.** All taxon claims in [6, 7, 8, 24, 27] are relative-abundance claims. Our 16S data are compositional; qPCR scaling is exploratory.
- **No responder phenotypes, no microbiome-based prediction of response.** [4] states plainly that a framework for predicting microbiota change from fibre structure still "needs to be developed." Deehan's consistency [5] was *within assigned treatment groups* in a controlled human trial, not a validated donor-level predictor.
- **No clinical, glycemic, or obesity-treatment inference.** [2] and [3] discuss glycemic and disease endpoints in *host* systems. Our ex vivo design measures microbial functional output and cannot speak to host outcomes.
- **No claim that carbohydrate restructured the community.** Community-level restructuring claims require the design and duration of [24] or [13]; a 48 h batch incubation with donor-dominated variance [25] does not license them.
- **Do not assert a uniform "SCFA increase."** The evidence base predicts analyte-specific responses [5, 9, 25]; report per-analyte and say so.

## Design translation caveats (paragraph drafted for Discussion 4.2)

> Our design deliberately removes the host from the substrate-delivery pathway, and this reframes rather than simplifies interpretation. The rapid- and slow-digesting descriptors applied to these formulations are host-enzyme kinetic labels: the RDS/SDS/RS classification is defined by the rate of pancreatic and amyloglucosidase hydrolysis, and only the resistant fraction was ever proposed as a guide to the quantity of starch reaching the colon (Englyst et al., 1992; Zhang & Hamaker, 2009). In an in vivo dietary comparison such as the rodent study that motivated this work (Plaza-Díaz et al., 2022), the small intestine acts as a filter, so the colonic microbiota encounters an undigested residue whose mass and chemistry differ from the ingested product. In an ex vivo fecal incubation that filter is absent, and even a consensus pre-digestion step cannot restore it, since the standardised static INFOGEST protocol holds meal-to-fluid ratio and pH constant within each phase and is explicitly stated by its developers to be unsuitable for simulating digestion kinetics (Brodkorb et al., 2019) — precisely the property that distinguishes a slow- from a rapid-digesting formulation. Consequently our contrast should be read as a test of how directly applied carbohydrate formulations differ as microbial substrates, not as a test of whether slower host digestion delivers more fermentable material to the colon. Three further features of the batch format constrain inference. First, closed batch incubations accumulate acid, and falling pH is itself a community-restructuring variable: mildly acidic conditions suppress *Bacteroides* and favour *Eubacterium rectale*-related butyrate producers, and lower pH shifts the stoichiometry of butyrate formation toward greater acetate uptake (Duncan et al., 2009; LaBouyer et al., 2022), so carbohydrate effects and acid-accumulation effects on butyrate are partially entangled. Second, the basal medium is itself a fermentable background; systematic optimisation work shows that a more nutrient-dense medium measurably compresses the metabolite signal attributable to an added fibre (Poppe et al., 2023), so any attenuated or null carbohydrate effect must be interpreted against medium richness rather than read as biological absence. Third, substrate supply does not translate linearly into short-chain fatty acid yield: utilisation is gated by the presence of appropriate primary degraders and cross-feeding partners (Ze et al., 2012; Louis & Flint, 2017), dose-response saturates at nutritionally realistic intakes (Deehan et al., 2020), and across structurally diverse starches total short-chain fatty acid production has been observed to remain similar while acetate, propionate and butyrate molar ratios diverge (Giuberti et al., 2013). For these reasons we report and interpret each analyte separately, and we do not treat any single carbohydrate arm as establishing superior fermentability.

**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** [27] is a rodent in vivo study reporting fecal acetate and propionate; it is the motivation for the question, not a benchmark for our result.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Because in vivo dietary comparisons confound carbohydrate identity with host digestive filtering, we used an ex vivo fecal fermentation in which each donor's microbiota was exposed directly to the formulations, isolating the microbial component of the response."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported. Frames the design; asserts no directional outcome.

**SECTION:** Discussion 4.2 — why "slow-digesting" does not map onto ex vivo microbial exposure
**PRIOR CLAIM:** The SDC/RDC descriptors index small-intestinal hydrolysis kinetics; ex vivo application bypasses that step, and standard static pre-digestion protocols cannot reproduce digestion kinetics.
**CITATIONS:** Lead — [1] Englyst 1992 (PMID 1330528); [23] Brodkorb 2019 (10.1038/s41596-018-0119-1); [21] Hernalsteens 2021 (10.1016/j.foodres.2021.110743). Backup — [2] Zhang & Hamaker 2009 (10.1080/10408390903372466); [19] Singh 2022 (10.1002/bit.28206).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** A semi-dynamic or dynamic pre-digestion could partially recover kinetic differences [19, 21]; our design did not use one, which is a limitation rather than a flaw in the descriptors.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Because the rapid- and slow-digesting descriptors are defined by host enzymic hydrolysis rate rather than by colonic delivery, and because static pre-digestion protocols are explicitly unsuited to simulating digestion kinetics, our comparison tests how these formulations differ as directly applied microbial substrates rather than how differently they would be delivered to the colon in vivo."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported. This is a scope limitation and strengthens, rather than extends, the claim set.

**SECTION:** Discussion 4.2 — analyte-specific rather than uniform SCFA response
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Acetate, propionate and butyrate arise from distinct pathways in distinct taxa and are expected to respond differently to a change in carbohydrate substrate; total SCFA may be unchanged while molar ratios shift.
**CITATIONS:** Lead — [11] Louis & Flint 2017 (10.1111/1462-2920.13589); [9] Giuberti 2013 (10.1017/S1751731113001092); [5] Deehan 2020 (10.1016/j.chom.2020.01.006). Backup — [12] Reichardt 2014 (10.1038/ismej.2014.14); [14] LaBouyer 2022 (10.1017/gmb.2022.1).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Acetate and butyrate are coupled through acetate uptake during butyrate formation [14, 11], so they are not fully independent endpoints; multiplicity adjustment across three analytes is still appropriate.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Because propionate and butyrate are generated by distinct pathways in distinct taxa and butyrate formation consumes acetate, we analysed each analyte separately rather than assuming a uniform shift in total short-chain fatty acid output."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported. Justifies analytic strategy; states no direction of effect.

**SECTION:** Discussion 4.2 — the "more substrate → more SCFA" caution
**PRIOR CLAIM:** SCFA yield is not a monotonic function of substrate supply; background medium composition, pH drift, degrader availability and cross-feeding all gate the response, and dose-response saturates.
**CITATIONS:** Lead — [22] Poppe 2023 (10.3389/fmicb.2023.1198903); [13] Duncan 2009 (10.1111/j.1462-2920.2009.01931.x); [15] Ze 2012 (10.1038/ismej.2012.4). Backup — [18] Louis 2007 (10.1111/j.1365-2672.2007.03322.x); [5] Deehan 2020 (10.1016/j.chom.2020.01.006).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** [22] is a pectin-based optimisation study in a specific medium; the direction of the medium-richness effect is well argued but the magnitude is not directly transferable to our medium.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Short-chain fatty acid accumulation in a closed incubation is gated by more than substrate supply — the fermentable background medium, progressive acidification, and the presence of appropriate primary degraders and cross-feeding partners each constrain yield — so an attenuated difference between carbohydrate conditions should not be read as an absence of substrate-specific microbial activity."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported as a limitation. Note: this must not be deployed selectively to rescue a null in one direction only; state it as a general property of the assay.

**SECTION:** Discussion 4.3 — model-system contrast and donor variance
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Batch, continuous and multi-compartment systems (SHIME, TIM-2, ARCOL) support different inferences; donor identity is a major, analyte-specific variance component; and paediatric colonic conditions differ systematically from adult ones.
**CITATIONS:** Lead — [19] Singh 2022 (10.1002/bit.28206); [25] Pirkola 2023 (10.1038/s41598-022-26847-y); [26] Fournier 2022 (10.1007/s00253-022-12199-0). Backup — [24] Long 2020 (10.3920/BM2020.0006); [21] Hernalsteens 2021 (10.1016/j.foodres.2021.110743).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** [25] used only two donors, so it demonstrates that donor effects can be large but does not quantify population variance. [26] is a model-development study, not a paediatric clinical comparison.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Single-vessel batch incubation captures net accumulation over a fixed window but cannot represent the compartmentalisation, retention time and pH control of continuous multi-compartment systems, and prior work using identical substrate doses across donors shows that donor identity can dominate the response and can do so differently for each short-chain fatty acid."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported as a limitation.

**SECTION:** Discussion 4.3 — relationship to the rodent anchor
**PRIOR CLAIM:** The originating rodent study reported higher fecal acetate and propionate under slow-digestion carbohydrate replacement within a high-fat diet; our ex vivo human design is not a replication of it.
**CITATIONS:** Lead — [27] Plaza-Díaz 2022 (10.3389/fnut.2022.992682). Backup — [21] Hernalsteens 2021 (10.1016/j.foodres.2021.110743); [1] Englyst 1992 (PMID 1330528).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Species, exposure duration (4 weeks vs 48 h), dietary background (high-fat diet vs defined medium), and the presence/absence of host digestion and absorption all differ. Butyrate was not among the elevated analytes reported there.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "A rodent high-fat-diet study reporting higher fecal acetate and propionate when rapidly digestible carbohydrate was replaced with a slowly digestible source motivated the present work; differences in species, exposure duration and the presence of host digestion mean our ex vivo findings neither confirm nor contradict that result."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported. Explicitly refuses both confirmation and contradiction, which is the honest position.

**SECTION:** Discussion 4.3 — donor-group comparison (obesity vs healthy weight)
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Donor-group × substrate batch-culture designs have precedent, but a non-significant group difference does not establish equivalent fermentation capacity.
**CITATIONS:** Lead — [28] Gerasimidis 2022 (10.3390/nu14051053) *— design precedent only*. Backup — [25] Pirkola 2023 (10.1038/s41598-022-26847-y); [22] Poppe 2023 (10.3389/fmicb.2023.1198903).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** ⚠ [28] concluded comparable fermentative capacity in IBD vs controls. That conclusion must not be imported as support for an obesity-equivalence claim in our study; our design includes no equivalence test and n=8 per group.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Prior batch-culture studies have compared fermentation across donor groups defined by clinical status; in the present study a non-significant group term should be interpreted as an absence of detected difference at this sample size, not as demonstrated equivalence."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** **Unsupported if used beyond this wording.** Any phrasing implying "preserved fermentation capacity" in the obesity group exceeds the evidence and violates the claim lock.
