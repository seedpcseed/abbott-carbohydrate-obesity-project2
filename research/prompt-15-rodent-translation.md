# Prompt 15 — Translation limits from rodent diet-replacement studies of rapid vs slow/resistant carbohydrates to human fecal ex vivo fermentation

**Manuscript target:** Discussion 4.2; Introduction paras 3–4 · **Citation job:** Establish the anchor rodent study as *biological rationale / hypothesis generator*, not a replication target, and supply the design-difference argument that blocks direct claim transfer · **Date:** 2026-07-19

> **Scope note.** This report was assembled under the project claim lock. It does **not** retrieve or construct support for SDC superiority over RDC, for obesity-group SCFA equivalence, or for reframing our nonsignificant SDC–RDC head-to-head as a successful translation of rodent superiority claims. ## 1. What the anchor paper actually claimed (read directly from `research/fnut-09-992682.pdf`)

Plaza-Díaz et al. 2022 (Front Nutr 9:992682; PMID 36532542) fed **30 weanling male Wistar Han rats (21–25 days old, n = 10/group) for 4 weeks** in three arms:

| Arm | Diet |
|---|---|
| NOB | AIN93G standard rodent diet (non-obese reference) |
| OBE | High-fat diet (HFD) with **rapidly** digestible carbohydrate |
| OBE-ISR | HFD with **slowly** digestible carbohydrate ("ISR") |

**ISR blend composition (Table 1, g/100 g CHO):** isomaltulose 26.40, Sucromalt® 22.10, maltodextrins 34.50, resistant maltodextrin 10.00, inulin:FOS 7.00. Estimated glycemic load 338 (ISR) vs 687 (HFD) vs 726 (AIN93G).

**A design detail with direct bearing on our study** (read from Table 1 and easy to miss): both HFDs list *total fiber* 16.00 g/100 g CHO, but in the plain HFD that fiber is **cellulose** (16.00), while in HFD-ISR cellulose is absent and the fiber allotment is made up of **resistant maltodextrin (10.00) + inulin:FOS (7.00)**. The rodent contrast is therefore **not a pure digestion-rate manipulation** — it simultaneously substitutes a non-fermentable bulking fiber for two highly fermentable colonic substrates. This is the single most important reason the rodent SCFA result cannot be read as a prediction about our RDC-vs-SDC head-to-head.

### Claims made (microbiome)
- α-diversity **decreased** in OBE-ISR (Fisher's alpha p = 0.004; species richness reduced; Pielou's evenness increased p = 0.004). Note the direction: the "beneficial" arm had *lower* richness.
- β-diversity separated OBE-ISR from NOB and OBE (weighted UniFrac ADONIS p = 0.001, r² = 0.495; unweighted p = 0.001, r² = 0.429).
- Genus-level: **increased** *Bifidobacterium*, *Bacteroides*, *Butyricimonas*, *Lachnoclostridium*, *Flavonifractor*, *Ruminiclostridium 5*, *Faecalibaculum*, *Alistipes*; **decreased** *Muribaculum*, *Blautia*, *Ruminiclostridium 9*. Genera *Tyzzerella* and *Angelakisella* were elevated in OBE and restored to NOB levels by ISR.
- Several of these are **rodent-associated taxa** (*Muribaculum*, *Faecalibaculum*, *Angelakisella*, *Tyzzerella*) with no direct human counterpart of comparable abundance.
- ***Fusicatenibacter* is not reported anywhere in the anchor paper.** No rodent prior exists for our exploratory *Fusicatenibacter* observation.

### Claims made (fecal SCFA — Table 5, µg/g dry feces, mean ± SEM)
| SCFA | NOB | OBE | OBE-ISR | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acetate | 1292.0 ± 335.7ᵃ | 609.7 ± 134.2ᵃ | 3388.0 ± 670.9ᵇ | ISR higher (p < 0.05) |
| Propionate | 785.3 ± 218.2ᵃ | 529.1 ± 159.8ᵃ | 1604.0 ± 256.6ᵇ | ISR higher (p < 0.05) |
| Butyrate | 351.9 ± 81.1ᵃ | 478.3 ± 125.8ᵃ | 618.8 ± 110.1ᵃ | **No significant difference** |

**This is load-bearing for our Discussion.** The anchor paper's own butyrate contrast was null. The rodent literature therefore never established a butyrate advantage for slow-digesting carbohydrate, and our nonsignificant SDC–RDC butyrate difference is not a failure to reproduce a rodent butyrate claim — there was no such claim to reproduce.

### Claims made (function and host)
- PICRUSt2-**predicted** (not sequenced) pathways increased in OBE-ISR: glycolysis with Entner–Doudoroff, glucuronic acid degradation, D-galacturonate degradation, N-acetylglucosamine/mannosamine/neuraminate degradation, pentose phosphate pathway, fatty acid biosynthesis, TCA, D-glucarate degradation; **decreased**: methanogenesis, methanogenesis from acetate, L-tyrosine degradation.
- Host outcomes were **not measured in this paper** — they are carried over from the companion study (Manzano et al. 2022, PMID 35425792), which reported that SDC substitution in growing rats limited adiposity, improved HOMA-IR, and altered GLUT4/GLUT2/FAS/SREBP1/AMPK/Akt signalling. A related earlier rodent paper (Martin et al. 2018, PMID 30253280) reported maternal-SDC effects on offspring adipogenesis.
- Funding/COI: supported by Abbott Laboratories S.A.; several authors are Abbott employees; the funder was involved in study design and sample collection.

### Nearest related animal claims, in one line
Across this rodent programme the claim structure is: *slow-digesting carbohydrate substitution within an HFD shifts the caecal/fecal community, raises fecal acetate and propionate, shifts predicted carbohydrate-metabolism pathways, and co-occurs with a better host metabolic phenotype in growing rats.* Every element of that chain depends on an intact host that digests, absorbs, and responds — which is exactly the layer our ex vivo design removes.

## 3. Prior-claim bullets (mapped to citation numbers)

**On what the animal work claimed**
- Replacing rapidly digestible with slowly digestible carbohydrate inside an HFD altered rodent fecal community structure and raised fecal acetate and propionate at 4 weeks; **butyrate did not differ** [1].
- The same substitution reduced α-diversity in the treated arm, so "improvement" in that paper is a compositional-shift claim, not a richness claim [1]; an analogous diversity reduction with increased soluble fiber has been reported in mice [16].
- Host metabolic benefit (adiposity, HOMA-IR, GLUT4/AMPK/Akt signalling) is claimed in a **separate** companion rodent study, not in the microbiome paper [2], with a further maternal-programming precedent [3].
- Functional pathway claims in the anchor are **16S-predicted** (PICRUSt2), and predictive accuracy for such tools is sample-type and function-category dependent, degrading notably outside human datasets [17].

**On why the rodent result cannot be a replication target for our design**
- Rodent-to-human microbiome inference is systematically over-called: 95% of human-microbiota-associated rodent studies reported phenotype transfer, a rate the authors judge implausible [4]. Animal findings are, in general, not reliable predictors of human outcomes and should not be positioned as confirmatory antecedents [21].
- Murine and human core gut microbiota overlap only partially; several anchor-paper responder genera (*Muribaculum*, *Faecalibaculum*, *Angelakisella*, *Tyzzerella*) are rodent-associated taxa without abundant human counterparts [1, 5].
- Rodent GM phenotypes are strongly conditioned by vendor, facility, cage, and diet matrix, which limits their portability even between rodent laboratories [6, 16].
- **The exposure is not the same molecule-to-microbe exposure.** Isomaltulose — 26.4% of the ISR carbohydrate blend — is 95–99% digested and 94–96% absorbed in the human small intestine [7]. In vivo it is a host-glycemic manipulation that barely reaches the colon. In a fecal ex vivo system that bypasses host digestion, it is presented intact to the community. In vivo "slow digestion" and ex vivo "substrate availability" are different independent variables [7, 12].
- **The rodent contrast confounds digestion rate with fermentable-fiber substitution.** The HFD fiber allotment is cellulose; the HFD-ISR allotment is resistant maltodextrin plus inulin:FOS at equal declared total fiber [1]. Diet matrix and fiber type are known to dominate rodent GM outcomes [16], so the fecal acetate/propionate rise is not attributable to digestion rate alone.
- **Fecal SCFA in a rodent ≠ net SCFA accumulation in a sealed vessel.** Fecal SCFA concentration is a residual of production, cross-feeding, and absorption [9]; systemic availability of colonic SCFA is 36% acetate, 9% propionate, 2% butyrate, with the remainder consumed or oxidised [8]; and fecal content differs metabolomically from the caecal fermentation site itself, with SCFAs among the main discriminators [18]. Our 0→48 h net accumulation is a different quantity with a different denominator.
- **Time-scale and kinetics differ.** A 4-week in vivo intervention permits community succession, host feedback, mucosal selection, and coprophagic re-inoculation; a 48 h batch incubation permits none of these [10, 11]. Substrates that are nominally "slow" in a digestive sense frequently ferment rapidly and to completion within a 48 h batch window [15] — an entirely sufficient mechanism for a rapid-vs-slow distinction to be absent ex vivo.
- **Donor identity is a first-order determinant of ex vivo outcome.** Fermentation capacity and substrate utilisation differ markedly between donor communities given identical substrate [13], and SCFA responses to the same fiber diverge by donor [14]. A between-substrate null in a donor-paired human design is therefore an interpretable statement about substrate effect size relative to donor variance, not a technical failure.
- **The donor population is developmentally distinct.** Adolescent gut microbiota differs from both children and adults [19, 20]; weanling Wistar rats aged 21–25 days are not a model of human adolescence in any microbial-compositional sense.
- **The assay is nonetheless responsive.** Validated human in vitro gut models detect macronutrient-driven metabolic and compositional shifts within 24 h [22], which is why a nonsignificant SDC–RDC contrast is informative rather than merely uninformative.

## 5. Discussion paragraph scaffold (§4.2)

*Four-move structure. Bracketed slots are for your measured values; every sentence is written to survive the claim lock.*

**Move 1 — name the rodent work as rationale, not antecedent result.**
> This study was motivated by a rodent diet-replacement experiment in which substituting slowly for rapidly digestible carbohydrate within a high-fat diet was associated with a shifted caecal-derived fecal community and higher fecal acetate and propionate in growing rats [1], alongside a favourable host metabolic phenotype reported separately [2]. That work supplied the **biological rationale** for asking whether human colonic communities themselves distinguish between these two carbohydrate formulations. It did not supply a result that the present design could reproduce, because the two designs estimate different quantities.

**Move 2 — the digestion-bypass argument (the strongest single point).**
> The rodent manipulation acted on an intact digestive tract. Its principal slow-digesting component, isomaltulose, is 95–99% digested and 94–96% absorbed in the human small intestine [7], so in vivo it functions largely as a modifier of glycemic delivery rather than as a colonic substrate. Our assay deliberately bypasses host digestion, presenting the formulations directly to the fecal community [12]. "Slow digestion" in vivo and "substrate availability to the microbiota" ex vivo are therefore distinct independent variables, and a contrast that exists on the first axis need not exist on the second.

**Move 3 — the confound and the endpoint mismatch.**
> Two further features limit comparison. First, the rodent slow-carbohydrate arm did not vary digestion rate in isolation: at equal declared total fiber, non-fermentable cellulose was replaced by resistant maltodextrin and inulin:fructooligosaccharides [1], and fiber type is a dominant determinant of rodent community and metabolite outcomes [16]. Second, the endpoints are not the same measurement. Fecal short-chain fatty acid concentration reflects the residual of production, microbial cross-feeding, and colonic absorption [9], with systemic availability of colonic acetate, propionate, and butyrate estimated at 36%, 9%, and 2% respectively [8], and fecal content differs metabolomically from the fermentation site itself [18]. Our endpoint is net accumulation over 48 h in a closed vessel with no absorptive sink — a related but non-interchangeable quantity.

**Move 4 — state the null accurately and set the correct evidentiary frame.**
> Against this background, we observed no significant difference between the slow- and rapidly-digesting formulations in net accumulation of acetate, propionate, or butyrate over 48 h [insert effect sizes and CIs]. We interpret this as evidence about the magnitude of a **direct substrate effect on adolescent fecal communities under ex vivo conditions**, and not as evidence for or against the rodent findings, which were generated in a system that retains host digestion, absorption, and a four-week window for community succession. We note that the anchor study's butyrate contrast was itself null [1], so no rodent butyrate advantage was ever established for this comparison. In a batch system, nominally slowly fermentable substrates frequently ferment rapidly and to completion well inside a 48-h window [15], which offers a parsimonious account of why an in vivo digestion-rate distinction would not be expected to persist here. Donor identity is a first-order determinant of ex vivo fermentation outcome [13, 14], and our within-donor paired design was constructed to place substrate effects against that variance rather than to detect a pre-specified superiority. Findings from dietary microbiome studies in rodents are best treated as **generators of testable human hypotheses** rather than as replication targets [4, 5, 21]; the present result speaks to one such hypothesis — that these formulations differ in their direct fermentability by adolescent colonic communities — and does not support it at the sampled effect size.

## 6. Conflicts / nuance

- **The anchor is not uniformly "pro-SDC."** α-diversity and species richness *decreased* in the treated arm [1]. If cited as a benefit narrative without that detail, the framing is inaccurate. A murine parallel exists in which increasing soluble fiber progressively reduced diversity [16].
- **Directional agreement on butyrate is coincidental, not corroborative.** Both the anchor and our study report a null butyrate contrast, but from incomparable designs and estimands. Do not present this as convergent validation — it would smuggle in the very superiority frame the claim lock excludes.
- **The acetate/propionate rise in the rodent study is real but multiply-caused.** Digestion rate, fermentable-fiber substitution, glycemic load (338 vs 687), and a four-week succession window all covary. Attributing it to digestion rate alone is unsupported by that paper's own design.
- **Absence of contrast ex vivo is not evidence of equivalence in vivo, and vice versa.** Both directions of inference are blocked. State the limitation symmetrically.
- **Ex vivo systems are demonstrably sensitive**, responding to macronutrient composition within 24 h [22], so the null is not attributable to assay insensitivity in general — but this argument must be made about our assay's demonstrated dynamic range (e.g. the no-added-carbohydrate arm), not asserted from the literature alone.
- **Funding symmetry.** The anchor and its companion were Abbott-supported with Abbott author affiliations [1, 2]. Our study shares a sponsor. Neutral framing of the rodent work as rationale-only is the safer and more defensible position.
- **PICRUSt2 caveat.** Do not import the anchor's pathway claims (Entner–Doudoroff, PPP, methanogenesis) as mechanism. They are 16S-inferred, and inference accuracy varies by sample type and functional category, degrading outside human data [17].
- **No *Fusicatenibacter* prior exists.** The genus appears nowhere in the anchor paper. Any *Fusicatenibacter* observation in our data must be framed as exploratory and unanchored, with no claim that it is an SDC utiliser or the butyrate source.

## 7. What we must not overclaim (given claim lock)

- ✗ Do **not** write that our study "translates," "extends," "supports," or "is consistent with" the rodent SDC benefit. It does none of these.
- ✗ Do **not** convert the shared null butyrate result into convergent evidence for SDC.
- ✗ Do **not** state or imply SDC superiority over RDC for acetate, propionate, or butyrate on any endpoint.
- ✗ Do **not** claim obesity-group equivalence or "preserved fermentation capacity" — nothing in this citation set speaks to that, and the design cannot establish it.
- ✗ Do **not** attribute host glycemic, insulin-sensitivity, adiposity, or obesity-treatment benefit to RDC/SDC from this design; those endpoints live only in the companion rodent paper [2].
- ✗ Do **not** cite *Bifidobacterium*, *Butyricimonas*, or *Faecalibaculum* changes from the anchor as expected human responses; several are rodent-associated taxa.
- ✗ Do **not** present PICRUSt2-predicted pathways from the anchor as measured mechanism.
- ✗ Do **not** claim *Fusicatenibacter* is an SDC utiliser or butyrate producer — no anchor prior, no citation in this set.
- ✓ **Do** state plainly that the rodent study is the originating rationale, that the designs estimate different quantities, and that our result addresses a direct-substrate-effect hypothesis in adolescent fecal communities under ex vivo conditions.

**SECTION:** Discussion 4.2 — digestion bypass
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Host digestion removes most of the anchor study's slow-digesting carbohydrate before the colon, so ex vivo exposure is not the in vivo colonic exposure.
**CITATIONS (lead):** [7] PMID 20211041; [12] PMID 30886367
**CITATIONS (backup):** [10] PMID 36166347

**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported, with the component-specific caveat retained.

**SECTION:** Discussion 4.2 — endpoint non-equivalence
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Rodent fecal SCFA concentration and our ex vivo net accumulation are different estimands.
**CITATIONS (lead):** [8] PMID 27510655; [9] PMID 24642959; [18] PMID 41002948
**CITATIONS (backup):** [11] PMID 23768557
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Boets et al. quantify systemic availability of *administered* colonic SCFA in adults, which bounds but does not directly measure absorptive loss during endogenous fermentation. Phrase as "estimated systemic availability," not "absorption fraction."
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Fecal short-chain fatty acid concentrations represent the residual of production, cross-feeding, and colonic absorption [9], with estimated systemic availability of colonic acetate, propionate, and butyrate of approximately 36%, 9%, and 2% [8], and fecal content differs metabolomically from the fermentation site itself [18]; net accumulation in a closed ex vivo vessel is therefore a related but non-interchangeable measurement."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported.

**SECTION:** Discussion 4.2 — interpreting the null
**PRIOR CLAIM:** A nonsignificant between-substrate contrast in a donor-paired 48-h batch design is interpretable, given known donor dominance and known rapid completion of nominally slow substrates.
**CITATIONS (lead):** [15] PMID 22417432; [13] PMID 36596824; [14] PMID 38798771
**CITATIONS (backup):** [22] PMID 26499094
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** These support a mechanistic account of why a contrast may be absent; they do **not** establish equivalence, and must not be used to argue the substrates are biologically interchangeable.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Nominally slowly fermentable substrates frequently ferment rapidly and to completion within a 48-h batch window [15], and donor community composition is a first-order determinant of ex vivo fermentation outcome [13, 14]; the absence of a detectable difference between formulations in this system should therefore be read as a bound on the direct substrate effect relative to donor variance, not as evidence of biological equivalence."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported — explicitly refuses the equivalence reading, which is a claim-lock non-goal.
