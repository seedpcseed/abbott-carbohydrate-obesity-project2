# Prompt 14 — Genus *Fusicatenibacter*: taxonomy, ecology, and the butyrate-production question
**Manuscript target:** Discussion 4.6; Results 3.7–3.8 · **Citation job:** Establish what is and is not known about *Fusicatenibacter*, and constrain wording for an exploratory relative-abundance SDC>RDC signal · **Date:** 2026-07-19

> **HEADLINE FINDING (read before drafting anything):** The original species description of *Fusicatenibacter saccharivorans* (Takada et al. 2013) reports the type strain's fermentation end products from glucose as **lactic acid, formic acid, acetic acid, and succinic acid — butyrate is not among them.** No isolate-level study retrieved in this search demonstrates butyrate production by any *Fusicatenibacter* strain. The widespread secondary-literature description of this genus as a "butyrate producer" is **not traceable to primary data**, and in at least one case (Wikipedia's *Fusicatenibacter* entry) the claim is miscited directly to Takada 2013, which says the opposite. Do not cite this genus as a butyrate producer.

## Prior-claim bullets (mapped to citation numbers)

**Taxonomy and description**
- *Fusicatenibacter* is a genus of Gram-positive, obligately anaerobic, non-spore-forming, spindle-shaped bacteria in the family **Lachnospiraceae**, erected in 2013 for *F. saccharivorans*; it falls within the *Blautia coccoides* rRNA group / Clostridium cluster XIVa. Nearest neighbours are only ~93.7% 16S-similar, i.e. it is a genuinely distinct lineage, not a renamed *Clostridium*. [1]
- The type strain is HT03-11ᵀ (= YIT 12554ᵀ = JCM 18507ᵀ = DSM 26062ᵀ). [1]
- **Reported fermentation end products from glucose: lactate, formate, acetate, succinate.** [1]
- A second species, *F. faecihominis*, has since been validly published; three further *Candidatus* taxa are listed. [⚠ unverified item]

**Ecology and prevalence**
- *Fusicatenibacter* is a common, reliably detected member of the adult human faecal community, recovered consistently enough across independent cohorts to appear as a recurrent differential taxon in systematic reviews of PD (≥3 studies), depression (44 studies), IgA nephropathy, cognition, and bone density. [9, 10, 15, 13]
- No study retrieved reports a formal population-level prevalence or carriage rate for the genus. Its "commonness" is inferred from its recurrence in differential-abundance lists, not from a dedicated prevalence survey.

**Carbohydrate responsiveness**
- Resistant maltodextrin supplementation roughly doubled faecal *F. saccharivorans* by targeted qPCR at both 15 and 25 g/day in healthy adults — the single strongest and most quantitatively rigorous carbohydrate-response result. [4]
- FOS-fructan challenge increased *Fusicatenibacter* relative abundance in a randomised crossover trial, **with no accompanying change in faecal SCFA**. [5]
- *In vitro*, *Fusicatenibacter* relative abundance shifted with xylan substrate structure while total SCFA was unchanged. [6]
- The species epithet *saccharivorans* ("sugar-devouring") reflects saccharolytic capacity in the original description [1]; it is **not** evidence of starch or resistant-starch utilisation specifically. No CAZyme/amylolytic characterisation of *Fusicatenibacter* was retrieved.

**SCFA / butyrate**
- The only quantitative link between *Fusicatenibacter* and SCFA output on our class of substrates is a **community-level correlation** in *ex vivo* batch fermentation of faecal microbiota from cirrhotic patients and controls. [3]
- The genus does not appear as a demonstrated butyrate producer in the canonical genomic pathway catalogue framework. [7]
- Where butyrate does arise from starch fermentation, the established mechanism runs through *R. bromii*-type primary degraders feeding `but`-gene-carrying secondary fermenters — a role not demonstrated for *Fusicatenibacter*. [8]

**Human metabolic association**
- Directionally **inconsistent**: enriched in adolescent obesity+NAFLD [11] and in osteoporosis [13]; depleted in depression [10], PD [9], and longevity cohorts [12]; positively associated with cognition [15] and with immunotherapy survival [14].

## Conflicts / nuance

1. **The central conflict: primary vs. secondary literature on butyrate.** Takada et al. 2013 [1] measured fermentation end products directly and found no butyrate. A large downstream literature nonetheless describes the genus as butyrogenic — sometimes explicitly, sometimes by inclusion in a list of "butyrate-producing genera" [10, 15]. We traced one instance (the Wikipedia *Fusicatenibacter* entry, accessed 2026-07-19) in which the claim "a butyrate-producing anaerobe" is **cited directly to Takada 2013**, the very paper that reports lactate/formate/acetate/succinate. This is a documented citation-provenance failure. Our manuscript must not join that chain.
2. **Guilt by family association.** Lachnospiraceae contains many well-characterised butyrate producers, and Clostridium cluster XIVa is a classic butyrogenic clade. Family membership is a hypothesis generator, not evidence about a genus. Vital et al. [7] make exactly this point: the butyrate-producing guild is polyphyletic and cannot be inferred from 16S taxonomy.
3. **A plausible, unproven alternative role.** The type strain produces lactate, formate and acetate [1]. Those are canonical **substrates** for butyrogenic cross-feeders. A cross-feeding/substrate-supplier role is at least as consistent with the data as direct butyrate production — and is equally unproven. Mention only as a hypothesis if at all.
4. **Correlation is at the community level.** Jin et al. [3] correlated taxon abundance with community SCFA output across donors; three taxa from two different families co-correlated. This design cannot attribute SCFA to any one taxon, and the authors do not claim it does. This is precisely the inferential limit our own donor-level Spearman analysis also sits behind.
5. **Compositional response ≠ functional response.** Two independent carbohydrate studies [5, 6] found *Fusicatenibacter* shifting while SCFA did not. This is the single most useful nuance for our Discussion: our own pattern (relative-abundance signal without qPCR-scaled confirmation and without a significant taxon–butyrate association) is **the expected and previously observed pattern**, not an anomaly requiring a mechanistic explanation.
6. **Metabolic-association direction is unresolved, and reverses in our age group.** The most demographically comparable study — adolescents with obesity [11] — found *F. saccharivorans* **higher** in those with NAFLD. Any implication that more *Fusicatenibacter* is metabolically favourable in adolescents is contradicted by the closest available evidence.
7. **Relative vs. absolute abundance.** The only study using absolute-style quantification (qPCR) [4] is also the only one showing a clean carbohydrate-driven increase. Our qPCR-scaled models did **not** reproduce our relative-abundance signal. Framing this as a discrepancy with [4] is honest; framing [4] as support for our finding is not.
8. **The strongest mechanistic result in the genus is immunological, not metabolic.** Takeshita et al. [2] showed IL-10 induction and colitis amelioration — an immune mechanism. Importing that paper to support an SCFA claim would be a category error.

## What we must not overclaim (given claim lock)

- ❌ Do **not** call *Fusicatenibacter* a butyrate producer, a "known butyrate producer," a "butyrogenic taxon," or a member of "butyrate-producing genera." No primary evidence supports this; the type-strain description contradicts it.
- ❌ Do **not** state or imply that *Fusicatenibacter* is the mechanistic source of any butyrate difference between SDC and RDC.
- ❌ Do **not** describe *Fusicatenibacter* as expanding, blooming, increasing in absolute terms, or being enriched in numbers. Our qPCR-scaled analysis did not support absolute change. Use "higher relative abundance" only.
- ❌ Do **not** describe *Fusicatenibacter* as a demonstrated SDC utiliser, starch degrader, or amylolytic organism. `saccharivorans` means saccharolytic in the original glucose-based characterisation; no starch-utilisation data exist for the genus.
- ❌ Do **not** present the donor-level rho=0.477 as evidence of a taxon–butyrate link. Permutation p=0.088 and a bootstrap CI crossing zero at N=14 make this a non-significant exploratory correlation.
- ❌ Do **not** chain [1] → [3] → [8] into an implied mechanism ("described as saccharolytic Lachnospiraceae → correlates with SCFA → starch fermentation yields butyrate"). Each link is real; the chain is not.
- ❌ Do **not** invoke [2] (IL-10/UC) in any SCFA, metabolic, or obesity context.
- ❌ Do **not** frame the *Fusicatenibacter* result as a candidate biomarker, responder marker, or basis for dietary-response prediction.
- ✅ **Do** state plainly that the taxon lacks demonstrated butyrogenic capacity, and that this is a reason for caution rather than an incidental caveat.

## EVIDENCE GRADES

| Domain | Grade | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Ecology & prevalence in the human gut** | **Moderate** | Consistently recovered across many independent cohorts and multiple systematic reviews [9, 10, 13, 15]; formally described with deposited type strain [1]. Downgraded from strong because no dedicated prevalence/carriage survey was found — commonness is inferred from recurrence in differential-abundance lists. |
| **Butyrate production** | **ABSENT** (for direct production); **Weak** (for any association with butyrate) | Original description reports lactate, formate, acetate, succinate — **no butyrate** [1]. No isolate, pure-culture, genomic (`but`/`buk`), or flux evidence retrieved. The only butyrate-adjacent data are community-level correlations [3]. Secondary sources asserting butyrogenesis are miscited or unsupported [10, 15, Wikipedia]. |

Responds to resistant maltodextrin by qPCR [4] and to FOS-fructans [5] and xylans [6] by relative abundance. | **Human metabolic association** | **Weak** (and directionally inconsistent) | Associations exist and are numerous, but conflict in direction: increased in adolescent obesity+NAFLD [11] and osteoporosis [13]; decreased in depression [10], PD [9], and longevity [12]; increased with better cognition [15] and cancer survival [14]. All cross-sectional or observational; none establishes causality or a metabolic mechanism. |
| *(bonus)* **Immunomodulatory / IBD role** | **Moderate** | Takeshita et al. [2] combine human observational data, a murine colitis model with isolate administration, and *ex vivo* IL-10 induction from human LPMCs. Strongest causal evidence in the genus — but immunological, not metabolic. |

## Discussion-safe claim bullets

Use these verbatim or near-verbatim. Each is defensible against the citations shown.

- "*Fusicatenibacter* is a commonly detected member of the adult human faecal microbiota within the family Lachnospiraceae [1, 9, 10]."
- "The type strain of *Fusicatenibacter saccharivorans* was described as saccharolytic, producing lactate, formate, acetate and succinate from glucose; butyrate was not reported among its fermentation end products [1]."
- "Although *Fusicatenibacter* is frequently grouped with butyrate-producing taxa in the secondary literature, we are not aware of isolate-level, genomic, or flux evidence establishing butyrogenic capacity for this genus."
- "*Fusicatenibacter* abundance has been shown to respond to fermentable carbohydrates in humans, including a roughly two-fold increase in faecal *F. saccharivorans* by qPCR following resistant maltodextrin supplementation [4], and increased relative abundance following fructo-oligosaccharide challenge [5]."
- "Notably, compositional shifts in *Fusicatenibacter* have been reported without accompanying changes in short-chain fatty acid concentrations, both in a randomised crossover trial [5] and in *in vitro* faecal fermentation [6]."
- "Community-level correlations between *F. saccharivorans* abundance and SCFA output in *ex vivo* fermentation of non-digestible carbohydrates have been reported [3], but such designs cannot attribute SCFA production to an individual taxon."
- "Human observational associations for *Fusicatenibacter* are inconsistent in direction, including higher abundance in adolescents with obesity and NAFLD [11] and in osteoporosis [13], alongside lower abundance in depression [10] and Parkinson's disease [9]."
- "Where butyrate production from starch has been mechanistically resolved, it involves primary degraders such as *Ruminococcus bromii* supplying substrate and acetate to secondary fermenters carrying butyryl-CoA:acetate CoA-transferase [8]; *Fusicatenibacter* has not been characterised in either role."

## Downstream synthesis blocks

### Block A

**SECTION:** Discussion 4.6 — interpreting the exploratory *Fusicatenibacter* signal

**PRIOR CLAIM:** *Fusicatenibacter* is a common human gut Lachnospiraceae genus whose abundance responds to fermentable carbohydrate, but whose fermentative output is characterised as lactate/formate/acetate/succinate rather than butyrate, and whose compositional responses have repeatedly been observed without concurrent SCFA change.

**CITATIONS (lead):** [1] Takada 2013 (10.1099/ijs.0.045823-0) · [4] Mai 2022 (10.3390/nu14112192) · [5] Herfindal 2024 (10.1186/s12916-024-03562-1)
**(backup):** [6] Zhao 2021 (10.1016/j.carbpol.2021.118418) · [3] Jin 2019 (10.1111/liv.14106)

**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Much secondary literature labels the genus butyrogenic without primary support, in at least one traceable case miscited to [1] itself. Family membership in Lachnospiraceae is not evidence of butyrate production [7]. The genus's reported end products (lactate, acetate, formate) are substrates for butyrogenic cross-feeders, making an indirect role plausible but entirely unproven.

**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:**
> "Relative abundance of *Fusicatenibacter* was higher under SDC than RDC at 48 h in exploratory differential-abundance testing, although this signal was not reproduced in qPCR-scaled models after correction for multiplicity. *Fusicatenibacter* is a commonly detected Lachnospiraceae genus whose abundance responds to fermentable carbohydrates in human intervention studies [4, 5], but whose type strain was described as producing lactate, formate, acetate and succinate rather than butyrate [1], and for which isolate-level evidence of butyrogenic capacity is lacking. Compositional shifts in this genus have previously been reported without concurrent changes in short-chain fatty acid concentrations [5, 6]. We therefore regard this observation as a hypothesis-generating compositional signal rather than an indication of a butyrate-producing mechanism."

**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** **Supported / exploratory.** Every element is either directly cited or explicitly flagged as exploratory. Contains no absolute-expansion, no utiliser claim, no butyrate-source claim.

### Block B

**SECTION:** Discussion 4.6 — the donor-level taxon–butyrate correlation

**PRIOR CLAIM:** Community-level correlations between taxon abundance and SCFA output do not establish that the taxon produces the SCFA.

**CITATIONS (lead):** [3] Jin 2019 (10.1111/liv.14106) · [8] Vital 2018 (10.1128/AEM.01562-18) · [7] Vital 2014 (10.1128/mBio.00889-14)

**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** [3] reports a positive *F. saccharivorans*–SCFA correlation on substrates including resistant starch, which superficially aligns with our rho=0.477. But that correlation is cross-donor and co-occurs with two taxa from a different family; it attributes nothing. Our own estimate is non-significant (permutation p=0.088; bootstrap CI crosses zero; N=14).

**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:**
> "The donor-level association between SDC-attributable change in *Fusicatenibacter* and net butyrate accumulation was positive but did not reach significance (Spearman ρ=0.477; permutation p=0.088; bootstrap confidence interval including zero; N=14). Cross-donor correlations of this kind, which have been reported previously for this taxon in *ex vivo* fermentation of non-digestible carbohydrates [3], cannot attribute short-chain fatty acid production to an individual taxon, since butyrogenesis from starch typically involves cross-feeding between primary degraders and secondary fermenters carrying the terminal butyrate-forming enzymes [7, 8]. We did not perform isolate or flux experiments and cannot resolve which community members generated the observed butyrate."

**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** **Supported.** Reports the non-significant result as non-significant; explicitly disclaims attribution and states the missing experiments.
