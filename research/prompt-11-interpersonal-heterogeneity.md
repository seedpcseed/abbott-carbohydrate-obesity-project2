## Prior-claim bullets (mapped to citation numbers)

**1. Donor identity is a first-order determinant of SCFA output from a fixed carbohydrate substrate.**
- Identical substrates fermented with different human faecal inocula yield different SCFA amounts and different acetate:propionate:butyrate ratios [2, 3, 4].
- The magnitude of between-person variation in butyrate specifically can approach an order of magnitude, even while a substrate main effect remains detectable [13].
- Across a 77-study synthesis, between-donor and between-study variability is large enough that substrate rankings for individual SCFAs are not stable [6].
- → *Our ICCs of 0.63–0.84 sit inside the "moderate-to-good" reliability band [23] and are consistent with, but do not extend, this literature.*

**2. Donor effects on composition are larger and more consistent than donor effects on SCFA endpoints — partial functional redundancy.**
- Reichardt et al. found compositional responses to be strongly donor- and substrate-specific while SCFA production was "surprisingly reproducible," attributing this to functional redundancy [1].
- Yao et al., in the largest factorial design to date, found donor variation expressed in *which* taxa were promoted but *not* in biomass or metabolite profiles, and framed the gut microbiota as having "conserved emergent properties" [5].
- Pirkola et al. observed donor differences that persisted for butyrate but converged for acetate and propionate by 24 h [3].
- → *This is the most important nuance for §4.5: donor effects on SCFA are real but partially damped relative to donor effects on composition.*

**3. Substrate-specific responder phenotypes are biologically possible and have been demonstrated — but only for chemically discrete substrates.**
- Two structurally distinct RS4 preparations directed propionate versus butyrate divergently within the same individuals [10].
- Community-type (Prevotella- vs. Bacteroides-dominated) modifies which fibres a donor ferments well [2].
- The hierarchical-specificity framework predicts that *low*-specificity (structurally simple, broadly utilised) substrates give divergent individual responses, while *high*-specificity substrates give convergent ones [7, 8, 9].
- → *Structurally related substrates should therefore rank donors more similarly than structurally divergent ones. Our RDC/SDC ρ ≈ 0.83–0.96 is consistent with this prediction; it is not a test of it.*

**4. Cross-substrate concordance of donor ranking is an under-tested construct and has never been validated as a person-level trait.**
- No located study formally estimates a cross-substrate rank correlation of SCFA response as a defined parameter with a confidence interval; the closest designs report qualitative concordance [1, 4] or fibre-by-individual interaction in vivo [11].
- Cantu-Jungles et al. treat across-individual consistency as a property *of the substrate*, not of the person [7, 8, 9].
- → *We should present ρ ≈ 0.83–0.96 as a descriptive, hypothesis-generating observation about these two carbohydrates, explicitly not as evidence of a generalized human "fermenter type."*

**5. Median-split responder/non-responder labels are statistically unsound as phenotypes.**
- Dichotomising a continuous outcome destroys information and power; data-derived cutpoints additionally introduce serious bias [20, 21].
- Within-subject random variation alone can produce the entire appearance of individual response differences; true individual response requires comparing change-score SDs across arms, which needs a comparator arm and/or within-subject treatment replication [16, 18].
- Selecting or classifying on a measured value guarantees regression to the mean, and the effect grows with measurement error [22].
- Empirically, applying different responder-classification rules to the same 20 subjects agreed for only 11 — a disagreement rate closely matching our 7–12 of 16 [15].
- Apparent responder subgroups routinely fail to replicate [19].
- → *Our A/B disagreement is therefore best presented as evidence that median-split labelling is unstable in our data, exactly as this literature predicts — not as a finding about donors.*

**6. Continuous-phenotype framing is the defensible forward path; prediction is not yet established.**
- Reviews of microbiome-based dietary-response prediction identify responder/non-responder classification as an unsolved problem requiring predictive-feature discovery and validation [24, 25].
- A credible prediction claim requires large derivation cohorts, independent validation, and prospective testing [26].
- → *We can advocate continuous modelling and pre-registered replication as future work without claiming any classifier or dietary predictor.*

## Conflicts / nuance

1. **Redundancy vs. individuality — the central tension.** Reichardt [1] and Yao [5] argue that SCFA endpoints are buffered by functional redundancy despite compositional individuality; Chen [2] and Pirkola [3] emphasise real donor differences in SCFA output. Our data (moderate-to-good ICCs, i.e. neither ~0 nor ~0.95) sit between these poles and should be presented as such rather than as support for either extreme.

2. **High cross-substrate ρ has at least three explanations, and our design cannot separate them.** (a) A genuine generalized fermentative capacity; (b) shared donor-level scaling factors — inoculum viable biomass, baseline SCFA pool, transit-related community state — that inflate correlation for any two substrates; (c) substrate similarity, i.e. RDC and SDC being close enough on the specificity hierarchy [7, 8] that they recruit largely overlapping taxa. Explanation (b) is a nuisance-variance account, not a phenotype account, and must be named.

3. **ICC magnitude is design-dependent, not a biological constant.** ICC is a ratio; with only two carbohydrate conditions and no within-donor replication of the *same* condition, donor variance and donor-by-run variance are not separable [17, 18]. Our ICCs are descriptive summaries of ranking stability within this experiment, and would change under a different substrate panel.

4. **Ex vivo batch fermentation removes host absorption, transit, and mucosal cross-talk.** Donor ranking measured at 48 h in a closed vessel need not correspond to in vivo SCFA exposure; in vivo studies show fibre main effects with substantial residual individual variation [11, 13], but the mapping between the two settings is unestablished.

5. **"Responsiveness" may be an exposure artefact rather than a trait.** Habitual fibre intake moderates prebiotic response [14], so donors who rank high may simply be donors whose recent diet primed the relevant guilds. This is a state, not a phenotype, and it is not measured in our design.

6. **Venkataraman [12] is frequently mis-cited.** It documents variable responses; it does not validate discrete responder classes. If we cite it, we must cite it alongside [15, 16] so it is not read as endorsing classification.

7. **Regression to the mean is not hypothetical here.** With n = 16 and a single 48 h measurement per donor-condition, the median-split "responder" set is defined on a value containing analytical error; any re-measurement would be expected to reshuffle borderline donors [22]. The 7–12 of 16 agreement is fully compatible with this mechanism alone.

## Competing interpretations

| Interpretation | What would support it | What our data actually show | Verdict for §4.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Generalized fermentative responsiveness** — donors carry a stable, substrate-transcending capacity to generate SCFA | High cross-substrate ρ *plus* within-donor replication, *plus* replication in an independent substrate pair, *plus* stability across sampling occasions | High ρ (0.83–0.96) from a single occasion, two substrates, no within-condition replication | **Consistent with, not demonstrated.** State as the leading hypothesis and name what would test it. |
| **B. Shared nuisance scaling** — ρ is inflated by donor-level inoculum density, baseline SCFA pool, or run-level effects common to both conditions | ρ collapses after adjusting for total 16S copy number / baseline SCFA / batch | We scale by total bacterial 16S qPCR (secondary/exploratory), so partial adjustment exists but is not a formal test | **Live alternative. Must be stated explicitly.** This is the most likely reviewer objection. |
| **C. Substrate similarity** — RDC and SDC are close enough on the specificity hierarchy that overlapping guilds ferment both | ρ would fall substantially for a structurally distant substrate pair (e.g. rhamnose vs. β-glucan [1]) | Untested — we have only one substrate pair | **Live alternative**, and well-grounded theoretically [7, 8, 9]. |
| **D. Discrete responder/non-responder phenotypes** | Bimodality in the change-score distribution; stable classification across independent measurements and across rules | Median-split agreement of only 7–12 of 16 across two definitions — the signature of an unstable, arbitrary cut, matching [15] | **Actively contradicted by our own data.** Report as a negative control that argues *against* dichotomous framing. |
| **E. Pure measurement noise** — no true donor differences at all | ICCs near zero; change-score SD equal to analytical replicate SD | ICCs 0.63–0.84, i.e. clearly above the poor-reliability band [23] | **Unlikely**, but we should say we cannot fully exclude a noise contribution without within-condition replication [16]. |
| **F. Obesity-group structure explains donor ranking** | Group separation in ranks | Out of scope for this claim; group effects are addressed elsewhere and must not be smuggled in here | **Do not raise in §4.5.** Keep donor heterogeneity and group comparison separate. |

**Recommended stance for the manuscript:** present A as the hypothesis the data are *compatible* with, concede B and C as unresolved alternatives in the same paragraph, and use D's failure as the affirmative argument for continuous modelling. This ordering is what makes §4.5 defensible rather than promotional.

## What we must not overclaim (given claim lock)

- ✗ Do **not** state or imply that our study identifies, validates, or replicates a "high fermenter" / "low fermenter" phenotype.
- ✗ Do **not** state that RDC–SDC ρ demonstrates a generalized fermentative capacity. It is *consistent with* one; alternatives B and C are unexcluded.
- ✗ Do **not** present ICC values as heritability, as a trait estimate, or as evidence of temporal stability. We measured one occasion per donor.
- ✗ Do **not** use the A/B median-split disagreement as a finding *about donors*. It is a finding about the instability of median-split classification.
- ✗ Do **not** claim, hedge toward, or gesture at microbiome-based prediction of dietary response, a classifier, a diagnostic, a biomarker, or precision/personalized-nutrition utility. Citations 24–26 are bounding citations only.
- ✗ Do **not** claim SDC superiority, obesity-group equivalence, or any clinical/glycemic/obesity-treatment implication from donor heterogeneity.
- ✗ Do **not** attribute donor ranking to any named taxon (including *Fusicatenibacter*) in §4.5.
- ✓ **Do** report ICC model/type/definition per [23], and state that donor and donor-by-run variance are not separable in this design [18].
- ✓ **Do** frame continuous-phenotype modelling and pre-registered replication as *future work*, not as something this study accomplished.

### Block 3

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.5 — cross-substrate concordance (the ρ result)
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Structurally related carbohydrates are expected to recruit overlapping microbial guilds and therefore to rank donors more similarly than structurally divergent carbohydrates; cross-substrate concordance is a property of the substrate pair as much as of the donor.
**CITATIONS (lead):** [7] Cantu-Jungles & Hamaker 2020 (PMID 32071263); [8] Cantu-Jungles 2021 (PMID 34182773); [9] de Campos Costa 2026 (PMID 41069234)
**BACKUP:** [1] Reichardt 2017 (PMID 29192904); [11] Baxter 2019 (PMID 30696735)
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** No prior study reports a formal cross-substrate rank correlation of SCFA change with a confidence interval, so our ρ has no direct published comparator. High ρ is also compatible with shared donor-level scaling (inoculum density, baseline SCFA pool) rather than with a transferable capacity.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Donor rankings of acetate, propionate and butyrate change were strongly concordant between the two carbohydrate formulations (Spearman ρ 0.83–0.96). Because the two formulations are structurally related and were fermented from the same inoculum on the same occasion, this concordance is equally consistent with substrate similarity or with shared donor-level scaling, and we therefore treat it as a hypothesis-generating observation rather than evidence of a transferable fermentative capacity."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** **Exploratory, correctly labelled.** The sentence names the competing explanations in-line, which is what keeps it inside the claim lock.

### Block 4

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.5 — the median-split negative result (**centerpiece**)
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Median-split responder/non-responder classification is statistically unsound: it destroys information, imports regression-to-the-mean through selection on a measured value, and produces classifications that disagree markedly when different rules are applied to the same subjects.
**CITATIONS (lead):** [15] Hecksteden 2018 (PMID 29357481); [16] Atkinson & Batterham 2015 (PMID 25823596); [21] Royston 2006 (PMID 16217841)
**BACKUP:** [22] Barnett 2005 (PMID 15333621); [20] Altman & Royston 2006 (PMID 16675816)
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** None material — this is among the most settled positions in the applied-statistics literature. The only nuance is that dichotomisation may be defensible for a pre-specified, externally anchored, clinically meaningful threshold; a median is neither pre-specified nor externally anchored.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "When donors were dichotomised at the median into 'responder' and 'non-responder' groups using two alternative definitions, the two classifications agreed for only 7–12 of 16 donors. This instability closely mirrors reports from the exercise-physiology literature, in which competing responder-classification rules applied to the same participants agreed for only 11 of 20, and is the expected consequence of dichotomising a continuous outcome measured once per participant. We therefore report continuous change scores throughout and do not treat median-defined groups as phenotypes."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** **Supported.** Frames the disagreement as a methodological finding, explicitly declines phenotype interpretation. This sentence is the guardrail for the whole section.
