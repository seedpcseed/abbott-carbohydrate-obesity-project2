## Prioritized follow-up citation list (the checklist, in the order we should propose it)

Ordered by cost-to-information ratio. Each step is paired with the citation that establishes it as the recognised standard.

**Step 0 — Analytic consolidation (no new wet-lab data; do before anything else).**
0a. Re-test the *Fusicatenibacter* contrast across ≥2 additional differential-abundance methods and report the consensus [36].
0b. Report the q-value explicitly as an FDR-calibrated discovery threshold, not a significance threshold [37].
0c. Report the qPCR-scaled null and the donor-level null in the same paragraph as the relative hit [3, 38].

**Step 1 — Absolute quantification (cheapest true escalation).**
Exogenous spike-in calibration or flow-cytometric cell counting, plus taxon-specific qPCR for *Fusicatenibacter*, on archived fermentation material. Converts "relative shift" into a testable abundance claim [9, 10, 11].

**Step 2 — Isolate growth + substrate disappearance.**
Pure-culture *F. saccharivorans* (type strain DSM 26062 / JCM 18507) grown on the SDC and RDC preparations; quantify carbohydrate disappearance and fermentation end products. This is the minimum bar for "utilises the substrate" under commensal Koch's postulates [1, 12, 28]. Note in advance that the published end-product profile predicts lactate/formate/acetate/succinate, not butyrate [28].

**Step 3 — Isotope tracing in the intact community.**
¹³C-labelled SDC added to the same ex vivo donor-fecal system; RNA-SIP (or GroEL/protein-SIP) to identify which taxa assimilate SDC carbon, with paired ¹³C-labelled SCFA measurement to trace carbon into butyrate. This is the design that directly answers our question without requiring a monoculture assumption [17, 18, 19, 14].

**Step 4 — Metatranscriptomics.**
Sequence expressed function at 0/24/48 h; test whether butyrate-pathway genes (per the Vital catalogue) are transcribed and by whom, since abundance and expression are demonstrably decoupled [15, 20, 22].

**Step 5 — Defined co-culture / cross-feeding test.**
*F. saccharivorans* alone and in co-culture with a lactate-utilising butyrogen (e.g. *Anaerostipes*/*Eubacterium hallii*-type organisms) on SDC, with ¹³C-lactate tracing, following the Belenguer design [13, 16, 21].

**Step 6 — Community add-back / keystone test.**
Supplement low-*Fusicatenibacter* donor communities with the isolate and test whether SDC fermentation extent and butyrate output change — the *R. bromii* paradigm [12, 2].

**Step 7 — Independent replication and (only then) prediction.**
New donor cohort; pre-specified baseline predictor; nested cross-validation; held-out validation cohort; TRIPOD+AI-compliant reporting [31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 38].

**Cross-cutting integration:** analyse Steps 3–5 outputs with an explicit multi-omics triangulation protocol whose declared product is testable mechanistic hypotheses [39, 40], and score the accumulated evidence against adapted Bradford Hill / Koch's criteria before any causal language is adopted [1, 5, 6, 7].

## Prior-claim bullets (each mapped to citation numbers)

- **Differential abundance produces a catalogue of candidates, not causes.** Microbiome-wide association output is explicitly framed in the primary literature as a list requiring refinement before mechanistic study; triangulation across independent phenotype–microbe mappings is the recognised refinement step [2, 1].
- **The field has a documented over-claiming problem.** A systematic review found 95% of human-microbiota-associated rodent studies reported phenotype transfer, a rate the authors judge implausible and warn undermines credibility [3]. This is a positive reason to under-claim, not merely a stylistic preference.
- **Bradford Hill can be adapted to microbial systems, including multicausal ones.** Formal adaptations exist both for microbial ecology generally [5] and for a specific dysbiosis–disease question, where relative-abundance-based definitions were identified as a key weakness [6].
- **Relative abundance is not abundance.** Compositional data carry an instrument-imposed arbitrary total [8]; microbial load varies up to ten-fold between healthy individuals, and load-blind analyses generate artefactual taxon trade-offs and specifically "hamper attempts to link microbiome features to … metabolite concentrations" [9]. Load-corrected reanalysis has empirically changed which taxa survive as markers [10].
- **16S cannot establish butyrogenic capacity.** Butyrate production is polyphyletic across at least nine phyla and multiple pathways, and is stated in the primary literature to be undetectable by 16S rRNA gene sequencing [15, 16].
- **A taxon that rises on a substrate need not be the taxon degrading it, nor the one producing the metabolite.** Generalist/specialist glycan utilisation and dense cross-feeding networks decouple these [21, 16]; empirically, the dominant responding taxon in a 174-person fiber trial (bifidobacteria) was not the butyrogenic contributor [25].
- **Functional redundancy predicts weak, non-unique taxon–metabolite mappings.** Gene/functional composition is conserved while taxonomy varies widely [22] — so a genus-level association with a metabolite is expected to be unstable across donors.
- **SCFA concentrations have specifically resisted prediction from community structure.** Random-forest regression on 16S and metagenomic data explained at most 14% of variance in fecal acetate/propionate/butyrate [30].
- **Same-experiment feature selection inflates apparent performance to the point of manufacturing signal from noise.** On null data, CV-tuned classifiers reported <30% error in 18.5–38% of simulations while performing at chance on independent test data; nested CV is required [32]. Microbiome ML performance estimates are described as "likely overoptimistic" absent this discipline [31].
- **Genuine prediction claims are held to a documented architecture.** Development cohort → independent validation cohort → (ideally) blinded intervention, reported per TRIPOD+AI [35, 34].
- **A q<0.1 threshold is by construction a discovery threshold.** The q-value measures significance in terms of the false discovery rate; ~10% of hits at q<0.1 are expected false [37]. Compounding this, 14 DA methods applied to 38 datasets produced drastically different significant-feature sets [36].
- **The recognised mechanistic evidence classes, in ascending strength:** isolate growth on the substrate and substrate disappearance [1, 12]; isotope tracing of substrate carbon into taxa and into the product [17, 18, 19, 13, 14]; expression evidence that the pathway is transcribed [20, 15]; defined co-culture demonstrating cross-feeding [13, 12]; community add-back changing the functional output [12].
- ***Fusicatenibacter saccharivorans*, as originally described, does not produce butyrate.** All three type-strain isolates produced lactic, formic, acetic and succinic acid from glucose [28]. Existing causal work on this species targeted IL-10 induction and colitis amelioration via isolate administration, not SCFA output [29].

## Conflicts / nuance

1. **Fusicatenibacter's published end-product profile actively constrains us — in a useful direction.** Takada et al. [28] report lactate, formate, acetate and succinate from glucose. This is not merely "butyrate unproven"; it is positive evidence that the genus type strain is not a butyrogen. The honest and more interesting framing is that *Fusicatenibacter* is a plausible **cross-feeding donor** (lactate/acetate) to butyrogenic *Anaerostipes*/*E. hallii*-type organisms [13, 16]. This must be presented as a hypothesis requiring co-culture and isotope tests, never as a finding. Caveat: the type-strain profile does not exclude strain-level variation, which is precisely why isolate work on donor-derived strains is Step 2 rather than assumed.

2. **Total-16S qPCR scaling is a partial, not complete, load correction.** It is superior to relative abundance alone and its use should be stated as a strength. But 16S copy-number variation across taxa and DNA-extraction bias mean it is not equivalent to flow-cytometric cell counting [9] or spike-in calibration [11]. Our absolute-scale null should be described as "no evidence of expansion under qPCR-based load correction," not as "proof of no expansion" — an absence of evidence framing, with the n=16 power limitation acknowledged.

3. **Bradford Hill and Koch's postulates are not interchangeable, and neither transfers cleanly.** Koch's-style frameworks [1, 7] demand isolation and re-introduction — feasible for a single organism, awkward for a community function like net butyrate accumulation. Bradford Hill adaptations [5, 6] accommodate distributed, multicausal contributions but are weaker evidentially and were designed for observational epidemiology. Our situation (a community-level metabolic output, multiple possible contributing taxa, functional redundancy [22]) sits between them. The defensible position is to cite both and claim neither is satisfied.

4. **Ex vivo fermentation is the right design for functional phenotyping and the wrong design for causal attribution to a taxon.** In vitro fermentor studies are explicitly valuable because they control pH and substrate supply [23] — but the same literature concludes responses occur at strain/species level and are "not easily predicted a priori" [23]. Our system's strength (controlled community-level functional readout) is orthogonal to what taxon-level mechanistic claims require.

5. **Zeevi et al. [35] must be cited carefully.** It is the best-known demonstration of the *architecture* a prediction claim requires (n=800 → independent n=100 → blinded RCT). It is also the paper most likely to be misread as endorsing microbiome-based dietary-response prediction, which is a named guardrail. Cite it exclusively for the validation architecture; do not cite it for the substantive claim.

6. **Prior in vivo work is not uniformly negative on quantitative load–SCFA linkage.** Salonen et al. [26] found total 16S qPCR signal correlated positively with the three major SCFAs. This is a *community-load*–SCFA relationship, not a taxon–SCFA relationship, and should not be repurposed to support the latter. Our donor-level taxon–butyrate null is consistent with this distinction.

7. **The "no significant qPCR-scaled expansion" result may be underpowered rather than truly null.** With n=16 donors and high inter-individual variance [26], a modest absolute expansion could be missed. The correct treatment is to report the effect estimate with its interval and decline to claim either expansion or its absence — which is itself the argument for Step 1 (better absolute quantification) rather than for a stronger current claim.

## What we must not overclaim (given claim lock)

- Do **not** write that *Fusicatenibacter* "increased," "expanded," "bloomed," "was enriched," or "responded to SDC" without the qualifier *in relative abundance*, and without the immediately adjacent statement that qPCR-scaled abundance did not change significantly. [8, 9]
- Do **not** describe *Fusicatenibacter* as a butyrate producer, butyrogenic taxon, SCFA producer, or "known butyrate producer." The type-strain description reports lactate/formate/acetate/succinate [28]; 16S cannot establish butyrogenic capacity in any case [15].
- Do **not** describe *Fusicatenibacter* as an SDC utilizer, degrader, or "slow-digesting-carbohydrate specialist." No isolate growth, substrate-disappearance, or isotope data exist in this study. [1, 12]
- Do **not** attribute any portion of the observed butyrate accumulation to *Fusicatenibacter*, including via softened verbs ("may drive," "likely contributes," "appears to underlie"). Hypothesis language must be explicitly marked as hypothesis and paired with the experiment that would test it. [3, 4]
- Do **not** state or imply that *Fusicatenibacter* (or any baseline feature) predicts, forecasts, stratifies, or identifies SCFA responders. No held-out validation exists; concurrent same-experiment features cannot support prediction [32, 34]; and SCFA has specifically resisted prediction from community structure [30].
- Do **not** present the q<0.1 result as "significant." Use "exploratory," "discovery-level," or "FDR-calibrated at q<0.1," and note method-dependence. [36, 37]
- Do **not** use the word "mechanism," "mechanistic," "causal," or "drives" in connection with our own results. Reserve these words for describing the follow-up studies that would be required.
- Do **not** extend any of the above into SDC-superiority, obesity-equivalence, responder-phenotype, or clinical/glycemic claims (global guardrails).
- Do **not** cite the exemplar studies [12, 13, 24, 25] in a way that implies our finding is of the same evidentiary class. They should appear as the standard we are proposing to meet, not as company we are keeping.

## Downstream synthesis blocks

### Block A — §4.6

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.6 — Interpreting the exploratory *Fusicatenibacter* signal

**PRIOR CLAIM:** Differential-abundance results from compositional 16S data identify candidate taxa, not causes; a relative-abundance shift without a corresponding load-corrected change cannot be interpreted as population expansion, and genus-level 16S assignment cannot establish butyrogenic capacity.

**CITATIONS (max 3 lead + 2 backup):** Lead — Gloor et al. 2017 [8]; Vandeputte et al. 2017 [9]; Vital et al. 2014 [15]. Backup — Vieira-Silva et al. 2019 [10]; Nearing et al. 2022 [36].

**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Total-16S qPCR scaling is a partial load correction, not equivalent to cell counting or spike-in calibration; with n=16 the absolute-scale result is an absence of evidence rather than evidence of absence.

**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "*Fusicatenibacter* relative abundance was higher under SDC than RDC at 48 h at an exploratory false-discovery threshold (q<0.1); because 16S profiles are compositional and this shift was not accompanied by a significant change after total-16S qPCR scaling, we treat it as a relative-composition signal rather than evidence of population expansion, and note that genus-level 16S assignment cannot in any case establish butyrate-producing capacity."

**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported (methodological statements 8, 9, 15 are standard); the exploratory hit itself is reported as exploratory. No expansion, utilisation, or production claim made.

---

### Block B — §4.7

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.7 — What would be required to link a taxon to the observed butyrate response

**PRIOR CLAIM:** Establishing that a taxon utilises a substrate or produces an observed metabolite requires a defined evidence stack — isolate growth with substrate disappearance, isotope tracing of substrate carbon into the taxon and into the product, expression evidence, and defined co-culture — of which none is provided by community-level amplicon data.

**CITATIONS (max 3 lead + 2 backup):** Lead — Neville, Forster & Lawley 2017 [1]; Ze et al. 2012 [12]; Belenguer et al. 2006 [13]. Backup — Herrmann et al. 2017 [17]; Louis & Flint 2017 [16].

**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Koch's-style frameworks fit single organisms better than community-level metabolic outputs; adapted Bradford Hill criteria [5, 6] accommodate multicausality but carry weaker evidential weight. Neither is satisfied here.

**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Under commensal adaptations of Koch's postulates, attributing substrate utilisation or metabolite production to a specific taxon requires isolate growth on the substrate with quantified substrate disappearance, isotopic tracing of substrate carbon into that taxon and into the product, expression-level evidence that the relevant pathway is transcribed, and defined co-culture to distinguish direct production from cross-feeding — none of which is obtainable from community-level amplicon profiling, and none of which we performed."

**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported. This is a statement about method requirements, and it explicitly disclaims our own evidence.

---

### Block C — §4.7 (continued) — the cross-feeding hypothesis

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.7 — A specific, testable hypothesis for *Fusicatenibacter*

**PRIOR CLAIM:** *F. saccharivorans* was described as producing lactate, formate, acetate and succinate from glucose; these are established cross-feeding intermediates consumed by lactate-utilising butyrate producers, so a saccharolytic taxon can co-vary with butyrate without producing it.

**CITATIONS (max 3 lead + 2 backup):** Lead — Takada et al. 2013 [28]; Belenguer et al. 2006 [13]; Louis & Flint 2017 [16]. Backup — Cockburn & Koropatkin 2016 [21]; Baxter et al. 2019 [25].

**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** The type-strain profile does not exclude strain-level variation in donor-derived isolates; this is one reason isolate characterisation is proposed rather than assumed. Also, the hypothesis is unsupported by our own donor-level data, in which the taxon–butyrate association was not significant.

**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Because the *F. saccharivorans* type strain was described as producing lactate, formate, acetate and succinate rather than butyrate, and because lactate and acetate are established cross-feeding substrates for butyrate-producing anaerobes, one testable hypothesis is that a saccharolytic taxon of this kind could co-vary with butyrate without producing it; we did not test this hypothesis, and our donor-level *Fusicatenibacter*–butyrate association was not statistically significant."

**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Exploratory — stated explicitly as an untested hypothesis, with the contradicting internal null reported in the same sentence. Does not assert *Fusicatenibacter* as a butyrate source or SDC utilizer.

### Block D — §4.8

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.8 — Prediction, validation, and the limits of concurrent features

**PRIOR CLAIM:** Features selected and evaluated within the same experiment cannot support predictive claims; cross-validation applied to a tuned model yields substantially biased error estimates, and fecal SCFA concentrations have specifically resisted prediction from bacterial community structure.

**CITATIONS (max 3 lead + 2 backup):** Lead — Sze, Topçuoğlu et al. 2019 [30]; Varma & Simon 2006 [32]; Topçuoğlu et al. 2020 [31]. Backup — Collins et al. 2024 (TRIPOD+AI) [34]; Whalen et al. 2022 [33].

**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Zeevi et al. [35] is the standard illustration of a validated prediction architecture and may be cited for that architecture only — not as support for microbiome-based dietary-response prediction, which our design cannot address.

**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "We make no predictive claim: taxa identified within this experiment were both selected and evaluated on the same data, a procedure known to yield substantially optimistic performance estimates in the absence of nested cross-validation and an independent held-out cohort, and prior work has found that fecal short-chain fatty acid concentrations are poorly predicted from bacterial community structure."

**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported. Explicitly disclaims prediction; guardrail on "microbiome prediction of dietary response" respected.

### Block E — §4.8 / Conclusion

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.8 and Conclusion — Nomination framing and follow-up agenda

**PRIOR CLAIM:** The recognised productive use of an exploratory differential-abundance hit is to nominate candidates for targeted mechanistic follow-up, with the required experiments named; multi-omics integration frameworks explicitly describe their own output as mechanistic hypotheses to be tested experimentally.

**CITATIONS (max 3 lead + 2 backup):** Lead — Surana & Kasper 2017 [2]; Walter et al. 2020 [3]; Pedersen et al. 2018 [39]. Backup — Fischbach 2018 [4]; Schloss 2018 [38].

**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Nomination language is only credible if the specific follow-up experiments are named and the current evidence gap is stated; vague "warrants further study" phrasing reproduces the over-claiming pattern Walter et al. document.

**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "We therefore nominate *Fusicatenibacter* as a candidate for targeted follow-up rather than as a mechanistic explanation, and specify the experiments that would be required to evaluate it: spike-in-calibrated absolute quantification, isolate growth on the slow-digesting carbohydrate preparation with quantified substrate disappearance, ¹³C-substrate RNA- or protein-based stable isotope probing in the same ex vivo system with paired labelled short-chain fatty acid measurement, metatranscriptomic confirmation that butyrate-pathway genes are expressed, defined co-culture with lactate-utilising butyrate producers, and independent replication in a new donor cohort before any predictive claim is entertained."

**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported. Nomination only; every stronger claim is explicitly deferred to a named future experiment. No SDC-superiority, obesity-equivalence, responder-phenotype, or clinical claim.
