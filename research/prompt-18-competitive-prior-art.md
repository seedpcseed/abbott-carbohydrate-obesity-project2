## Positioning paragraph (drop-in draft, Discussion §4.2)

> Prior human work on digestion-rate-defined carbohydrates has proceeded along two tracks that have not met. On the host side, randomized crossover trials of slow- versus rapid-digesting carbohydrate formulations — including one in pre-pubertal children using the same formulation contrast studied here — have measured substrate oxidation, glycemia, and incretin responses, but have not assayed microbial endpoints (Gillen et al., 2021; Vanschoonbeek et al., 2009; Angarita Dávila et al., 2019). On the microbial side, ex vivo fecal fermentation has repeatedly shown that carbohydrate structure and digestion rate shape both community composition and short-chain fatty acid output (Rose et al., 2009; Kaur et al., 2011; Reichardt et al., 2018; Deehan et al., 2020), and that these responses differ substantially between donors (Martínez et al., 2010; Venkataraman et al., 2016; Blecksmith et al., 2025). Studies bridging donor phenotype and fermentation have generally pooled inocula by group (Aguirre et al., 2014; Vieira et al., 2021), which improves reproducibility at the cost of dissolving the between-donor variance that is arguably the main signal (Wang et al., 2026). The nearest neighbour to the present work is Holmes et al. (2020), who exposed fecal microbiota from 17 adolescents with obesity to five commercial prebiotics and found large, composition-linked inter-donor differences in acidogenic potential; that study, however, did not include a healthy-weight comparison group and did not test a digestion-rate-defined carbohydrate contrast. Our design occupies the remaining gap: each adolescent donor's community is incubated in parallel with no added carbohydrate, a rapid-digesting formulation, and a slow-digesting formulation, with donors sampled across healthy-weight and obesity strata, so that carbohydrate effect, donor identity, and weight stratum are estimable in the same model. We report this as functional phenotyping of microbial communities under a defined substrate contrast, not as evidence of digestive, glycemic, or obesity-treatment effect.

*(One-sentence compressed variant for Introduction ¶3: "Although slow- versus rapid-digesting carbohydrate formulations have been compared for host substrate oxidation in children, and ex vivo fermentation has been used to characterize adolescent obesity-associated microbiota against commercial prebiotics, no human study has applied the rapid-versus-slow-digesting carbohydrate contrast to paired ex vivo fermentations from the same adolescent donors across weight strata.")*

## Prior-claim bullets (mapped to citation numbers)

- **Carbohydrate digestion/fermentation *rate*, not only amount, changes late-window SCFA output and community pattern.** [8, 7, 9]
- **Chemically discrete carbohydrate structures can direct SCFA output toward particular acids in humans.** [11, 12, 6]
- **SCFA production is comparatively redundant across donors even where composition diverges sharply.** [6]
- **Yet fermentation outcomes are also demonstrably donor-specific and substrate-specific in combination.** [16, 13, 12, 14]
- **Ex vivo fecal fermentation is an accepted method for phenotyping fermentation *capacity* as a group-level trait (cases vs controls).** [15, 1]
- **Adolescents with obesity have microbiota that actively ferment carbohydrate substrates, with large inter-donor spread in acidogenic potential linked to community composition.** [1]
- **In lean-vs-obese ex vivo comparisons, results conflict: one BMI-stratified RS study reports similar SCFA output across BMI [4], while substrate-dependent lean/obese differences are reported elsewhere [5, 17].**
- **Fecal SCFA concentration, ex vivo SCFA production capacity, and obesity markers did not positively correlate with one another in adolescents with obesity.** [1]
- **Commercial multi-component slow-digesting blends (isomaltulose, sucromalt) have peer-reviewed human data — but exclusively on host glycemic/incretin endpoints.** [19, 20]
- **The RDC/SDC contrast has a published pediatric randomized crossover on host substrate oxidation, and a published rodent microbiome study, both Abbott-affiliated; neither is a human microbial study.** [2, 3]
- **Pooling fecal inocula reduces inter-sample variation and improves in vitro reproducibility — which is precisely why pooled designs cannot address donor repeatability.** [23]
- ***Fusicatenibacter saccharivorans* abundance has been reported to correlate positively with ex vivo SCFA production in a human case-control fermentation study — a correlation, not a demonstrated utilization or production role.** [15]

## Conflicts / nuance

1. **Functional redundancy vs donor specificity is genuinely unsettled.** Reichardt [6] found SCFA output "surprisingly reproducible" across donors despite compositional divergence; Blecksmith [16], Venkataraman [13], and Holmes [1] all emphasize donor-dependent responses. These are reconcilable — redundancy in *which* acids are made, variability in *how much* — but the manuscript should state the tension rather than pick a side.
2. **Lean-vs-obese ex vivo results conflict directionally.** Li [4] found similar SCFA across BMI; Aguirre [5] found differences whose direction *reversed* by substrate; Vieira [17] found lean > obese. Note also that [5] and [17] pooled inocula by group, so their group contrasts confound donor identity with group.
3. **"Slowly digestible" and "slowly fermentable" are not the same axis, and neither reliably matches its label.** Kaur [7] showed several nominally slow fibers ferment fast. A 48-h endpoint may therefore capture a fully completed fermentation for both arms, compressing any rate difference into a similar total. This is a substantive limitation for a 0→48 h net-accumulation primary endpoint and belongs in §4.3.
4. **Substrate identity differs from ours in every neighbour.** Holmes [1] used OTC prebiotics; the RS literature [4, 11, 12, 13, 14] used purified resistant starches. No prior human ex vivo study tested a multi-component commercial-style RDC vs SDC formulation pair.
5. **Batch pH is a hidden effector.** Reichardt [6] showed initial pH shifts the butyrate/propionate stoichiometry independently of composition. Any acetate/propionate/butyrate ratio we report is conditional on our buffering.
6. **Deehan [11] is the strongest "structure directs SCFA" claim, but it is an in vivo RCT with purified RS4s at up to 35 g/d.** It supports plausibility, not transfer, to an ex vivo mixed-formulation contrast.
7. **Sponsor overlap.** [2] and [3] share our sponsor. Cite them as motivating prior work with the affiliation stated in-text; do not let them carry the weight of independent corroboration.

## What we must not overclaim (given claim lock)

**Claims the prior literature makes that we are forbidden from copying:**

| SDC is **superior** to RDC for SCFA production | Not asserted by any retrieved study for an SDC-vs-RDC contrast — the closest is structure-directed *differences* in [11] | No prior work supports an SDC>RDC superiority claim. Do not import one by analogy from the RS literature. |

**Additional guardrails for §4.2–4.3:**
- Do not describe our 48-h Bray–Curtis result as carbohydrate-driven restructuring; [6] provides the safer framing (composition responds substrate-specifically *in some systems*) and our own result governs what we say.
- Do not use the word "prebiotic" for the RDC or SDC formulations — none of them has been evaluated against a prebiotic definition, and [1] reserves that word for products that have.
- Frame our donor-repeatability quantification as a **variance-decomposition** contribution, not as evidence that individual donors are stable "phenotypes."

## Gaps our study fills — and gaps it does not

**Fills:**
1. **First human ex vivo test of a digestion-rate-defined RDC-vs-SDC formulation pair on microbial SCFA output.** The RDC/SDC contrast exists in human hosts [2] and in rodent microbiomes [3], but the human microbial cell of that 2×2 is empty.
2. **Paired within-donor design across three arms (no-carbohydrate control, RDC, SDC).** Holmes [1] paired substrates within donor but had no weight-stratified comparison; Aguirre [5] and Vieira [17] had the weight contrast but pooled inocula away.
3. **Explicit quantification of donor repeatability / between-donor variance as a reported result rather than a nuisance.** The field's dominant response has been to pool it away [23] or to convert it into responder categories [13]; a variance-component treatment is a distinct and more conservative contribution.
4. **Adolescent donors across healthy-weight and obesity strata.** [1] covered adolescents with obesity only; [4] covered BMI strata in adults; [18] covered children without a stratified substrate contrast.
5. **A commercial-style multi-component slow-digesting blend taken to the microbial layer.** The commercial SDC-blend literature [19, 20] stops at host glycemia.

**Does not fill (state plainly in §4.3):**
1. **No in vivo confirmation.** Ex vivo batch fermentation removes host absorption, transit, mucosal cross-talk, and the small-intestinal digestion that defines the RDC/SDC distinction in the first place. Nothing here speaks to what happens in a child who drinks either formulation.
2. **No obesity-treatment, glycemic, or digestive claim.** [21] and [2] show what such designs look like; ours is not one.
3. **No equivalence testing.** A null group difference is not evidence of preserved capacity [4].
4. **No causal attribution to any taxon.** We cannot resolve which organisms produced which acid; [15]'s correlational framing is the ceiling.
5. **No rate resolution within the 48-h window.** [7], [8], and [9] all show the informative differences between slow and fast substrates live in the *shape* of the 0–48 h curve; a net-accumulation endpoint at 48 h cannot recover it.
6. **No dose-response.** [11] plateaued at 35 g/d; we test a single dose.
7. **No mechanistic link from formulation chemistry to outcome.** We do not characterize the linkage composition of the RDC/SDC blends the way [9] and [11] did for their substrates.

## Downstream synthesis blocks

**SECTION:** Introduction ¶3 (the gap statement)
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Digestion-rate-defined carbohydrate formulations have been compared in children for host substrate oxidation, and adolescent obesity-associated microbiota have been phenotyped ex vivo against commercial prebiotics, but these two literatures have not intersected.
**CITATIONS (lead):** Gillen 2021 (PMID 34130017); Holmes 2020 (PMID 32788375); Plaza-Díaz 2022 (PMID 36532542). **(backup):** Vanschoonbeek 2009 (PMID 19448045); Basuray/Deehan 2024 (PMID 38480843).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Gillen and Plaza-Díaz are Abbott-affiliated and must be identified as such in-text. Gillen enrolled healthy-weight children only (BMI 5th–95th percentile), so it does not itself establish an obesity contrast.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Slow- versus rapid-digesting carbohydrate formulations have been compared in pre-pubertal children for postprandial substrate oxidation, and the same contrast has been examined in the gut microbiome of diet-induced-obese rats; in adolescents, ex vivo fecal fermentation has been used to characterize how obesity-associated microbiota respond to commercial prebiotics. To our knowledge, the rapid- versus slow-digesting carbohydrate contrast has not been applied to paired ex vivo fermentations from human adolescent donors."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported — a gap statement, no efficacy or superiority implied.

---

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.2 (positioning against nearest prior art)
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Carbohydrate structure and digestion rate shape ex vivo SCFA output and community composition, with responses that are simultaneously partly redundant across donors and partly donor-specific.
**CITATIONS (lead):** Reichardt 2018 (PMID 29192904); Deehan 2020 (PMID 32004499); Blecksmith 2025 (PMID 40916941). **(backup):** Rose 2009 (PMID 18925612); Martínez 2010 (PMID 21151493).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Redundancy [6] and donor specificity [16] are stated most strongly by different studies using different donor numbers (3 vs 5) and substrates. Neither used a formulation-level RDC/SDC contrast.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Prior ex vivo work has shown that non-digestible carbohydrates of differing structure elicit distinct compositional responses while producing comparatively consistent short-chain fatty acid profiles across donors, and that fermentation nonetheless varies substantially between individuals; our results are consistent with a picture in which carbohydrate identity and donor identity both contribute to observed short-chain fatty acid accumulation."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported, provided no directional SDC>RDC statement is attached.

---

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.2 (donor repeatability as the contribution)
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Between-donor variation in fermentation is large enough that most designs either pool it away or reclassify it as responder status.
**CITATIONS (lead):** Venkataraman 2016 (PMID 27357127); Bendiks 2020 (PMID 32251948); Wang 2026 (PMID 41761056). **(backup):** Holmes 2020 (PMID 32788375); Aguirre 2014 (PMID 25426858).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Wang 2026 is a pig model — cite for the methodological principle of pooling only, not as human evidence. Venkataraman's responder tiers are prior work we describe, not a method we adopt.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Because inter-individual variation in carbohydrate fermentation is substantial, prior designs have frequently pooled fecal inocula or stratified participants post hoc by response magnitude; our paired design instead retains each donor as an experimental unit, allowing between-donor variation to be estimated alongside the carbohydrate effect."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported. **Would become unsupported** if extended to claim that our variance estimates identify responder phenotypes or predict dietary response.

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.3 (obesity-stratum limitation)
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Ex vivo comparisons of lean and obesity-associated microbiota have produced conflicting, substrate-dependent results.
**CITATIONS (lead):** Li 2015 (PMID 26256258); Aguirre 2014 (PMID 25426858); Vieira 2021 (PMID 33937924). **(backup):** Jin 2019 (PMID 30919578); Holmes 2020 (PMID 32788375).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Li reports similar SCFA across BMI; Aguirre reports differences whose direction reverses by substrate; Vieira reports lean > obese. Aguirre and Vieira pooled by group. Li's "benefit across BMIs" wording is an equivalence claim we cannot echo.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Previous ex vivo comparisons of microbiota from lean and obese donors have not converged, with reported differences depending on the substrate tested and several studies using group-pooled inocula; our study was not designed or powered to establish equivalence between weight strata, and any absence of a detected group difference should not be read as evidence that fermentation capacity is preserved."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported as written — the second clause is the explicit anti-equivalence guard the claim lock requires. **Unsupported** in any version that drops that clause.

---

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.3 (48-h window limitation)
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Differences between slowly and rapidly fermenting substrates are expressed in the shape of the fermentation time course, and many nominally slow substrates ferment rapidly.
**CITATIONS (lead):** Kaur 2011 (PMID 22417432); Rose 2009 (PMID 18925612); Gu 2018 (PMID 29710405). **(backup):** Reichardt 2018 (PMID 29192904).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** These are fiber/starch studies, not formulation studies; the extrapolation to a multi-component blend is by analogy. Gu's two-phase α-(1→4)-then-α-(1→6) pattern is the most directly transferable observation.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Because slowly and rapidly fermenting substrates are distinguished primarily by the timing rather than the eventual total of short-chain fatty acid production, a single net-accumulation endpoint at 48 hours may under-resolve rate differences between the formulations tested here."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported — a limitation, and it also pre-empts over-reading any null contrast.

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.2 (exploratory taxon framing)
**PRIOR CLAIM:** *Fusicatenibacter saccharivorans* abundance has been reported to correlate positively with ex vivo short-chain fatty acid production in human fecal fermentation.
**CITATIONS (lead):** Jin 2019 (PMID 30919578). **(backup):** Bendiks 2020 (PMID 32251948) for the general point that taxon-level responses to a single carbohydrate vary across studies.
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Jin's finding is a correlation in a cirrhosis case-control cohort, in a different disease context, with different substrates. It establishes that the association has been observed; it establishes nothing about utilization of an SDC formulation.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "*Fusicatenibacter* abundance has previously been reported to correlate with short-chain fatty acid production in human fecal fermentation, though the organism's role in carbohydrate utilization in this setting has not been established; our observation is exploratory and correlational."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported **only** with the "exploratory and correlational" qualifier and without any absolute-expansion language. **Unsupported** if written as *Fusicatenibacter* expanding, utilizing SDC, or producing the butyrate we measured.
