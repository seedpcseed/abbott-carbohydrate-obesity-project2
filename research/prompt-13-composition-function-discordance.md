## Prior-claim bullets (mapped to citation numbers)

- **B1 — Compositional change and SCFA output can move independently under carbohydrate/fiber exposure.** Documented in both directions: human dietary-fiber intervention changed community composition without a consistent fecal SCFA shift [2]; in vitro fermentation across donors produced reproducible SCFA profiles despite marked compositional divergence [1]; and enterotype-defined compositional strata showed no corresponding difference in metabolite profiles [3]. Systematic-review-level evidence confirms fiber interventions frequently move composition without moving individual SCFAs [16].
- **B2 — Functional redundancy is the standard ecological explanation offered for this discordance.** Taxon identity supporting a given function varies widely with little effect on the function [4]; redundancy is argued to underlie the stability and resilience of gut metabolic output under disturbance [5]; and human microbiome gene/functional capacity is far more conserved than taxonomic composition [6].
- **B3 — But redundancy must be invoked as an interpretation, not a demonstration.** Reduced functional variability can be produced by statistical averaging across aggregated taxa and does not by itself establish redundancy or functional selection [7]. A nonsignificant metabolite contrast is therefore consistent with redundancy but does not demonstrate it.
- **B4 — PERMANOVA is explicitly a variance-partitioning method, which licenses term-specific R² reporting.** The defining contribution of the method is additive partitioning of multivariate variation across the terms of a complex design [8], implemented on arbitrary dissimilarity measures such as Bray–Curtis via sequential/partial sums of squares [9]. Reporting one omnibus R² would discard exactly the information the method was built to provide.
- **B5 — Term-specific R² must be reported with the design structure and a dispersion caveat.** The framework accommodates hierarchical, mixed, and repeated-measures designs with restricted permutation [11] — the basis for permuting within donor. A significant term may reflect a centroid shift, heterogeneous dispersion, or both, so any group term should be paired with a dispersion test before being read as a location difference [10].
- **B6 — A small-but-nonzero obesity-group R² is the expected magnitude, not a weak version of a strong effect.** Pooled reanalysis across ten studies found the obesity–microbiome association real but "relatively weak" and swamped by interpersonal variation [12]; in deeply phenotyped population cohorts, individual host covariates each explain only a small share of compositional variance [13].
- **B7 — Time/incubation dominating the variance partition is consistent with prior in vitro work.** Residence/transit time was the single largest driver of both composition and metabolic activity in a validated in vitro gut model [14], and in batch fermentation one inoculum's communities clustered by fermentation time rather than substrate [15].
- **B8 — Inoculum/donor identity commonly outweighs substrate chemistry in ex vivo fermentation.** Starting inoculum had a greater effect than polysaccharide chemistry on fermentation outcomes [15]; donor microbiota differences shaped which taxa responded even where aggregate fermentation outcomes were consistent [3].
- **B9 — Null SCFA group contrasts are not evidence of equivalent fermentation capacity.** Measured SCFA pools reflect production, cross-feeding, and (in vivo) absorption jointly, and the obesity-direction literature is contested [17]; combined with the low power of small-donor designs to detect modest differences [12], absence of a detected difference must be reported as such.

## Conflicts / nuance

1. **Redundancy is contested as an inference, not as a phenomenon.** Refs [4–6] treat conserved function amid variable taxonomy as a real and general pattern; ref [7] shows the same signature can arise from statistical averaging in aggregated abundance data. **Resolution for our manuscript:** cite [4]/[6] for the ecological framing, [7] as an immediate limiting clause, and never state that our data demonstrate redundancy.
2. **Reichardt [1] cuts both ways.** The same paper reports *substrate-specific* stimulation of particular bacteria **and** reproducible SCFA output. It is therefore not a citation for "carbohydrate had no compositional effect" — it is a citation for "compositional specificity can coexist with convergent SCFA output." Note also that its SCFA reproducibility was partly pH-dependent, a variable our design does not manipulate.
3. **Feng [15] is inoculum-conditional.** In that study the *porcine* inoculum clustered by fermentation time while the *human* inoculum differentiated by substrate. Cite [15] for "inoculum > substrate chemistry" and for the existence of time-dominated clustering, but do not present it as evidence that human fecal communities never separate by substrate.
4. **Minnebo [14] measures transit time in a continuous SHIME system, not batch incubation duration.** The R² magnitudes are a loose comparator for our 0→48 h term, not a like-for-like benchmark. Frame as "consistent with in vitro systems in which temporal terms dominate," not as a matched effect size.
5. **Fecal vs. ex vivo SCFA pools are different quantities.** Ref [17] concerns fecal excretion in vivo, where absorption confounds interpretation; our endpoint is net accumulation in a closed incubation. Use [17] only to argue that SCFA pool sizes are not a clean readout of capacity — not to import its obesity-direction findings.
6. **Effect-size benchmarks from [12]/[13] come from cross-sectional human cohorts,** not donor-aggregated ex vivo designs with repeated measures. They calibrate the *expectation* that group terms are small; they do not validate our specific R².
7. **A dispersion alternative remains open for our group term** [10]: a modest group R² at 48 h could reflect greater between-donor spread in one group rather than a centroid separation. This must be checked and stated.

## What we must not overclaim (given claim lock)

- ❌ **Do not** cite any source above to claim that carbohydrate (RDC or SDC) drove 48 h Bray–Curtis restructuring. Our carbohydrate and group × carbohydrate terms were nonsignificant in claim-facing models; refs [1], [3], [15] describe *other* systems and substrates and cannot be transferred to license a carbohydrate effect here.
- ❌ **Do not** convert nonsignificant SCFA group contrasts into "preserved," "equivalent," or "comparable" fermentation capacity in the obesity group. Refs [7], [12], [16], [17] together make the correct statement one of *non-detection*, not equivalence. No equivalence-testing procedure was performed.
- ❌ **Do not** state that our data demonstrate functional redundancy. At most: our pattern is *consistent with* accounts in which shared metabolic capacity buffers SCFA output against compositional variation [4–6], with the caveat of [7].
- ❌ **Do not** use the modest group R² to claim a microbiome signature of adolescent obesity, a responder phenotype, or any predictive capability. Ref [12] explicitly reports poor cross-study classification accuracy.
- ❌ **Do not** cite the SDC-versus-RDC butyrate hypothesis from this file. Nothing here addresses it, and the file must not be used to support SDC superiority.
- ❌ **Do not** claim *Fusicatenibacter* or any taxon as the mechanistic source of any SCFA on the basis of the redundancy literature — [4] and [6] argue the opposite direction (taxon identity is weakly tied to function).
- ❌ **Do not** infer host, glycemic, or obesity-treatment relevance. Ref [17] is cited only for measurement interpretation, not for cardiometabolic inference from our design.

## Downstream synthesis blocks

### Block 1 — Term-specific PERMANOVA R² reporting

**SECTION:** Methods (statistical analysis) / Results §3.6
**PRIOR CLAIM:** PERMANOVA is a variance-partitioning procedure whose per-term R² values are the intended output for a multi-factor design, and the framework supports hierarchical/repeated-measures structure via restricted permutation.
**CITATIONS (max 3 lead + 2 backup):** Lead — [8] Anderson 2001 (10.1046/j.1442-9993.2001.01070.x); [9] McArdle & Anderson 2001 (10.1890/0012-9658(2001)082[0290:FMMTCD]2.0.CO;2); [11] Anderson 2017 (10.1002/9781118445112.stat07841). Backup — [10] Anderson & Walsh 2013 (10.1890/12-2010.1); [14] Minnebo 2023 (PMID 37926855).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** Sequential sums of squares make R² order-dependent, so term order must be stated explicitly [9]. Significance of any term is not attributable to location alone without a dispersion check [10].
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Because PERMANOVA additively partitions multivariate variation across the terms of a model rather than testing a single omnibus hypothesis (Anderson 2001; McArdle & Anderson 2001), we report R² separately for incubation time, participant group, carbohydrate, and their interactions, with permutations restricted within donor to respect the repeated-measures structure of the design (Anderson 2017), and we accompany the group term with a test of multivariate dispersion (Anderson & Walsh 2013)."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported — purely methodological; asserts no biological effect.

### Block 2 — Time dominates the variance partition

**SECTION:** Results §3.6 framing / Discussion §4.4
**PRIOR CLAIM:** In ex vivo and in vitro fermentation systems, temporal terms routinely dominate the partition of both community composition and metabolic activity.
**CITATIONS:** Lead — [14] Minnebo 2023 (10.1186/s40168-023-01691-y); [15] Feng 2020 (10.1039/d0fo01103g). Backup — [1] Reichardt 2017 (10.1038/ismej.2017.196).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** [14] manipulates continuous transit time in SHIME, not batch incubation duration; [15] shows time-dominated clustering in the porcine but not the human inoculum. Both are directional analogues, not matched benchmarks.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Incubation time accounted for the largest share of community variation in our model (R² ≈ 0.24), consistent with in vitro systems in which temporal terms are the leading driver of both composition and metabolic activity (Minnebo et al. 2023) and with batch fermentations in which communities cluster by fermentation time (Feng et al. 2020)."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported. Note the sentence attributes dominance to *time*, not to carbohydrate — this is the guardrail-critical distinction.

### Block 3 — Modest obesity-group community association alongside nonsignificant SCFA group interactions

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.4 (primary target)
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Obesity-associated compositional signals in fecal microbiota are real but small relative to inter-individual variation, and small group-level community terms coexist routinely with undetectable differences in metabolic endpoints.
**CITATIONS:** Lead — [12] Sze & Schloss 2016 (10.1128/mBio.01018-16); [13] Falony 2016 (10.1126/science.aad3503); [3] Yao 2024 (10.1016/j.carbpol.2024.122284). Backup — [10] Anderson & Walsh 2013 (10.1890/12-2010.1); [16] Vinelli 2022 (10.3390/nu14132559).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** [12] and [13] derive from cross-sectional human cohorts, not ex vivo repeated-measures designs. The group term may reflect dispersion rather than centroid separation until tested [10]. Non-detection is not equivalence; no equivalence test was performed.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Participant group accounted for a small but detectable share of community variation (R² ≈ 0.026 overall, rising to ≈ 0.055 at 48 h), an effect magnitude in line with reports that obesity-associated compositional signals are real but modest relative to inter-individual variation (Sze & Schloss 2016) and with population-scale estimates in which individual host covariates each explain only a small percentage of compositional variance (Falony et al. 2016). This modest structural association was not accompanied by detectable group differences in SCFA difference-in-change contrasts; a comparable dissociation — compositional strata that differ while fermentation metabolite profiles do not — has been reported in controlled in vitro fermentation (Yao et al. 2024). We emphasise that the absence of a detected SCFA group difference is a non-detection in a modestly powered design and is not evidence of equivalent fermentation capacity."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported, provided the final non-equivalence clause is retained. **Unsupported if used beyond evidence:** any phrasing such as "preserved fermentation capacity," "obesity groups fermented equivalently," or "comparable SCFA output" would breach the claim lock.

### Block 4 — Functional redundancy as interpretive frame for composition–function discordance

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.5 (primary target)
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Convergent SCFA output despite divergent community structure is a recurrent finding, commonly attributed to functional redundancy — an attribution that is itself contested on inferential grounds.
**CITATIONS:** Lead — [1] Reichardt 2017 (10.1038/ismej.2017.196); [4] Louca 2018 (10.1038/s41559-018-0519-1); [7] Ho & Huang 2025 (10.1016/j.cels.2025.101350). Backup — [6] Tian 2020 (10.1038/s41467-020-19940-1); [5] Moya & Ferrer 2016 (10.1016/j.tim.2016.02.002).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** [7] shows the statistical signature of redundancy can arise from averaging alone; [1] simultaneously reports substrate-specific taxonomic stimulation, so redundancy at the SCFA level does not imply compositional insensitivity. Our design does not measure functional gene content and therefore cannot test redundancy directly.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "A dissociation between community structure and SCFA output is well precedented: in vitro fermentation of diverse non-digestible carbohydrates produced reproducible SCFA profiles despite marked inter-individual compositional differences (Reichardt et al. 2017), and a high-fiber dietary intervention shifted gut microbiome composition without a consistent change in fecal SCFAs (Oliver et al. 2021). Such patterns are frequently interpreted through functional redundancy, in which the taxa encoding a metabolic function vary substantially with limited effect on the function itself (Louca et al. 2018; Tian et al. 2020). We note, however, that our design measured neither functional gene content nor community-level metabolic capacity, and that reduced variability in aggregated functional readouts can arise without genuine functional selection (Ho & Huang 2025); we therefore offer redundancy as a candidate interpretation rather than a demonstrated mechanism."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported as written (interpretation explicitly hedged). **Unsupported if used beyond evidence:** stating that our results demonstrate, establish, or confirm functional redundancy, or naming any taxon as the redundant functional unit.

### Block 5 — Null SCFA contrasts are common and are not equivalence

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.5 (limitations) / §4.4 close
**PRIOR CLAIM:** Interventions that measurably alter gut microbial composition frequently fail to produce detectable SCFA differences, and measured SCFA pools are an imperfect readout of fermentative capacity.
**CITATIONS:** Lead — [16] Vinelli 2022 (10.3390/nu14132559); [2] Oliver 2021 (10.1128/mSystems.00115-21); [17] de la Cuesta-Zuluaga 2018 (10.3390/nu11010051). Backup — [12] Sze & Schloss 2016 (10.1128/mBio.01018-16); [7] Ho & Huang 2025 (10.1016/j.cels.2025.101350).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** [17] concerns in vivo fecal excretion, where absorption confounds pool size; our closed-incubation net accumulation avoids the absorption confound but introduces its own (cross-feeding, substrate depletion, pH drift). Cite [17] for the general measurement caution only.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "Null SCFA contrasts alongside measurable compositional change are a common outcome in this literature rather than an anomaly: a systematic review of 44 human fiber intervention studies found no significant differences in individual SCFAs in the majority of studies despite documented microbiota modulation (Vinelli et al. 2022), and a controlled high-fiber intervention altered composition without a consistent SCFA shift (Oliver et al. 2021). Because measured SCFA concentrations reflect the joint outcome of production, cross-feeding and, in vivo, host absorption (de la Cuesta-Zuluaga et al. 2018), and because our donor-level sample size limits sensitivity to modest group differences, we interpret our nonsignificant group and group × carbohydrate SCFA contrasts as non-detection rather than as evidence of comparable fermentative output."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported. **Unsupported if used beyond evidence:** any restatement that drops "non-detection" in favour of "no difference," "similar," or "preserved."

### Block 6 — Donor/inoculum identity outweighing substrate

**SECTION:** Discussion §4.4 (supporting)
**PRIOR CLAIM:** In ex vivo fermentation, the identity of the donor inoculum frequently exerts a stronger influence on outcomes than the chemistry of the added substrate.
**CITATIONS:** Lead — [15] Feng 2020 (10.1039/d0fo01103g); [3] Yao 2024 (10.1016/j.carbpol.2024.122284). Backup — [1] Reichardt 2017 (10.1038/ismej.2017.196).
**CONFLICTS / NUANCE:** [15]'s human inoculum *did* differentiate by substrate; the inoculum-dominance conclusion is drawn across inoculum types. [3] found donor differences manifested in *which* taxa responded rather than in aggregate fermentation outcomes. Neither licenses a statement that carbohydrate had no effect in our system — only that donor structure is a strong competing source of variation.
**MANUSCRIPT-SAFE SENTENCE:** "The prominence of donor-level structure in our variance partition is consistent with ex vivo work in which the composition of the starting inoculum exerted a greater influence on fermentation outcomes than polysaccharide chemistry (Feng et al. 2020), and in which donor microbiota differences were expressed through which taxa responded to a given fibre rather than through aggregate metabolite profiles (Yao et al. 2024)."
**CLAIM-LOCK CHECK:** Supported. Guardrail: this block explains *why* the carbohydrate term is small relative to donor and time variation; it must not be rewritten to assert that carbohydrate shaped 48 h beta diversity.
