## Isolation

### Citation

Takada, T., Kurakawa, T., Tsuji, H., & Nomoto, K. (2013). *Fusicatenibacter saccharivorans* gen. nov., sp. nov., isolated from human faeces. *International Journal of Systematic and Evolutionary Microbiology*, *63*(Pt 10), 3691–3696. https://doi.org/10.1099/ijs.0.045823-0

### Summary

This paper describes the isolation and characterization of *Fusicatenibacter saccharivorans* gen. nov., sp. nov., a novel bacterium isolated from the feces of a healthy Japanese adult. The organism is a Gram-positive, obligately anaerobic, non-spore-forming, rod-shaped bacterium that belongs to the family Lachnospiraceae within the phylum Firmicutes. The species name *saccharivorans* reflects its ability to utilize various carbohydrates as growth substrates. The type strain HT03-11 was isolated using modified PY (Peptone Yeast extract) medium supplemented with carbohydrates. The bacterium exhibits saccharolytic activity, fermenting various sugars including glucose, arabinose, xylose, cellobiose, and starch. Genomic and phenotypic analyses confirmed that this bacterium represents a novel genus and species within the Lachnospiraceae family, which is abundant in the human gut microbiome and plays important roles in carbohydrate metabolism and short-chain fatty acid production.

### Isolation Methods

#### Sample Collection and Processing

1. **Sample Source**: Fresh feces from a healthy Japanese adult
2. **Sample Processing**: 
   - Dilute fecal sample in anaerobic diluent
   - Prepare serial dilutions for plating

#### Medium Preparation

**PY (Peptone Yeast extract) Medium - Base Composition:**

- **Peptone**: 10 g/L
- **Yeast extract**: 5 g/L
- **Sodium chloride (NaCl)**: 5 g/L
- **Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃)**: 2.5 g/L
- **L-cysteine hydrochloride**: 0.5 g/L
- **Hemin**: 5 mg/L
- **Vitamin K₁**: 10 µL/L
- **Resazurin**: 0.001% (w/v) as redox indicator
- **Distilled water**: to 1 L
- **pH**: Adjust to 6.8–7.0 before autoclaving

**PY + X Medium (Modified PY with Carbohydrate Supplement):**

To the base PY medium, add one or more of the following carbohydrates as growth substrates:

- **Glucose**: 10 g/L (optional, can be added separately to test specific fermentative capabilities)
- **Arabinose**: 5 g/L
- **Xylose**: 5 g/L  
- **Cellobiose**: 5 g/L
- **Starch**: 5 g/L

**Note**: The DSMZ catalogue indicates the strain is cultivated on medium 104c (PY + X Medium), suggesting a modified version of PY medium supplemented with specific carbohydrates.

#### Agar Medium Preparation

For solid media, add:
- **Agar**: 15 g/L (for plates)
- **Agar**: 10 g/L (for slants)

#### Sterilization and Preparation

1. Dissolve all components except carbohydrates, hemin, vitamin K₁, and cysteine in distilled water
2. Adjust pH to 6.8–7.0 with NaOH or HCl
3. Add resazurin indicator
4. Autoclave at 121°C for 15 minutes
5. Cool to 50–55°C under anaerobic conditions
6. Add filter-sterilized carbohydrates, hemin (dissolved in 1N NaOH), vitamin K₁ (ethanol solution), and L-cysteine hydrochloride
7. Pour plates or dispense into tubes in an anaerobic chamber

#### Cultivation Conditions

- **Temperature**: 37°C
- **Atmosphere**: Strictly anaerobic
  - **Gas composition**: 80% N₂, 20% CO₂
  - **Oxygen removal**: Use anaerobic chamber or anaerobic jar with gas-generating system
  - **Reducing agent**: L-cysteine hydrochloride (0.05% final concentration)
- **Incubation time**: 2–7 days
- **Medium**: PY + X Medium (104c) as per DSMZ specifications

#### Isolation Procedure

1. **Serial Dilution**: Prepare 10-fold serial dilutions of fecal sample in anaerobic diluent (phosphate-buffered saline with cysteine)
2. **Plating**: Spread 100 µL of appropriate dilutions onto PY + X agar plates
3. **Anaerobic Incubation**: Place plates in anaerobic jar or chamber at 37°C
4. **Colony Selection**: After 2–7 days, select colonies that appear on the selective medium
5. **Purification**: Subculture isolated colonies onto fresh PY + X plates to obtain pure cultures
6. **Characterization**: Test isolates for Gram staining, morphology, and biochemical characteristics

#### Key Characteristics of Isolates

- Gram-positive rods
- Obligately anaerobic
- Non-spore-forming
- Saccharolytic (ferments various carbohydrates)
- Produces short-chain fatty acids (likely butyrate and/or propionate)
- Optimal growth at 37°C, pH 6.8–7.0

#### Strain Identification

The type strain HT03-11 (DSM 26062, JCM 18507, YIT 12554) was identified through:
- 16S rRNA gene sequencing (GenBank accession: AB698910)
- DNA–DNA hybridization studies
- Phenotypic characterization (sugar fermentation, enzyme activities)
- Fatty acid analysis
- DNA G+C content determination (46.9 mol%)

### Summary

This paper describes a comprehensive culturomics approach to isolate and characterize previously uncultured human gut anaerobes, resulting in the discovery and description of several novel bacterial species, including *Fusicatenibacter faecihominis*. The study employed enhanced culturing techniques with multiple selective and non-selective media, including YCFA (Yeast extract, Casitone, Fatty acids) medium, to increase the diversity of culturable gut bacteria. Using fecal samples from healthy adults in Aachen, Germany, the authors isolated numerous previously uncultured strains, with *Fusicatenibacter faecihominis* type strain H1-YC4 (also designated as CLA-AA-H277, DSM 113288, JCM 35911) being one of the novel species characterized. The bacterium was isolated using YCFA-MEDIUM (MODIFIED) and identified through polyphasic taxonomy including 16S rRNA gene sequencing, whole-genome sequencing, and phenotypic characterization. This culturomics approach significantly expanded the cultured fraction of the human gut microbiome and revealed the metabolic potential of these newly isolated organisms.

### Isolation Methods

#### Sample Collection and Processing

1. **Sample Source**: Fresh feces from healthy adult human (male) in Aachen, Germany
2. **Sample Processing**:
   - Process samples immediately or store under anaerobic conditions
   - Homogenize fecal samples in pre-reduced anaerobic diluent (PBS with 0.05% L-cysteine hydrochloride)
   - Prepare serial 10-fold dilutions (10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁸) in anaerobic diluent
   - All processing conducted in anaerobic chamber or under N₂/CO₂ atmosphere

#### Medium Preparation

**YCFA-MEDIUM (MODIFIED) - Base Composition (DSMZ Medium 1611):**

This is a modified version of the original YCFA (Yeast extract, Casitone, Fatty acids) medium designed for culturing fastidious human gut anaerobes:

**Per Liter:**

- **Yeast extract**: 10 g
- **Casitone**: 10 g (pancreatic digest of casein)
- **Glucose**: 5 g
- **Cellobiose**: 5 g
- **Maltose**: 5 g
- **Starch (soluble)**: 5 g
- **Sodium chloride (NaCl)**: 4.5 g
- **Potassium dihydrogen phosphate (KH₂PO₄)**: 0.45 g
- **Dipotassium hydrogen phosphate (K₂HPO₄)**: 0.45 g
- **Magnesium sulfate heptahydrate (MgSO₄·7H₂O)**: 0.09 g
- **Calcium chloride dihydrate (CaCl₂·2H₂O)**: 0.09 g
- **Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃)**: 4 g
- **Ammonium sulfate ((NH₄)₂SO₄)**: 0.9 g
- **L-cysteine hydrochloride**: 0.5 g (reducing agent)
- **Hemin**: 5 mg (dissolved in 0.01 N NaOH)
- **Vitamin K₁ (Menadione)**: 0.5 mg (dissolved in 95% ethanol)
- **Resazurin**: 0.001% (w/v) as redox indicator (pink when oxidized)
- **Volatile fatty acid mixture**:
  - **Acetic acid**: 17 mM (1.02 mL of glacial acetic acid/L)
  - **Propionic acid**: 10 mM (0.74 mL/L)
  - **Butyric acid**: 4 mM (0.36 mL/L)
  - **Isobutyric acid**: 1 mM (0.09 mL/L)
  - **Valeric acid**: 1 mM (0.11 mL/L)
  - **Isovaleric acid**: 1 mM (0.11 mL/L)
- **Distilled water**: to 1 L
- **pH**: Adjust to 6.8–7.0 before sterilization (with NaOH if needed)

**Note**: The volatile fatty acids should be added after autoclaving and cooling to prevent evaporation during sterilization.

#### Agar Medium Preparation

For solid media:
- **Agar (bacteriological grade)**: 15 g/L (for plates)
- **Agar**: 10 g/L (for slants or deep agar)

#### Sterilization and Preparation

1. **Base Medium Preparation**:
   - Dissolve all components except volatile fatty acids, hemin, vitamin K₁, and cysteine in distilled water with gentle heating
   - Add resazurin indicator
   - Adjust pH to 6.8–7.0 with NaOH or HCl
   - For liquid medium, dispense into tubes or bottles leaving headspace
   - For agar medium, add agar and autoclave

2. **Sterilization**:
   - Autoclave at 121°C for 15 minutes
   - For liquid medium: Use anaerobic tubes or bottles with tight-fitting caps
   - For agar: Autoclave in flasks, cool to 50–55°C before adding heat-sensitive components

3. **Addition of Heat-Sensitive Components** (in anaerobic chamber or under N₂):
   - Filter-sterilize volatile fatty acids (mix all together, filter through 0.22 µm membrane)
   - Add volatile fatty acid mixture to cooled medium
   - Add hemin (5 mg/L, dissolved in 0.01 N NaOH, filter-sterilized)
   - Add vitamin K₁ (0.5 mg/L, dissolved in 95% ethanol, filter-sterilized)
   - Add L-cysteine hydrochloride (0.5 g/L, can be added before autoclaving or after)

4. **Reduction of Medium**:
   - Add L-cysteine hydrochloride if not added before sterilization
   - Allow medium to sit in anaerobic chamber for several hours to fully reduce (resazurin should turn colorless/pink when oxidized, colorless when reduced)
   - Alternatively, boil liquid medium briefly and cool rapidly under N₂/CO₂ flow

5. **Dispensing**:
   - Pour agar plates in anaerobic chamber or dispense liquid medium into anaerobic tubes
   - Pre-reduce media by storing in anaerobic chamber or jar overnight before use

#### Cultivation Conditions

- **Temperature**: 37°C (optimal for human gut bacteria)
- **Atmosphere**: Strictly anaerobic
  - **Gas composition**: 85% N₂, 10% H₂, 5% CO₂ (typical for anaerobic chambers)
  - **Alternative**: 80% N₂, 20% CO₂ for anaerobic jars
  - **Oxygen removal**: Use anaerobic chamber (glove box) or anaerobic jar with gas-generating systems (AnaeroGen™, AnaeroPack™)
  - **Catalyst**: Use palladium catalysts in anaerobic jars to remove residual O₂
- **Incubation time**: 3–14 days (some slow-growing anaerobes require extended incubation)
- **Medium**: YCFA-MEDIUM (MODIFIED) - DSMZ Medium 1611
- **pH**: 6.8–7.0 (maintained by bicarbonate buffer)

#### Isolation Procedure

1. **Sample Inoculation**:
   - Inoculate 100 µL of appropriate dilutions (typically 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻⁷) onto YCFA agar plates
   - Spread evenly with sterile glass spreader or beads
   - For enrichment, also inoculate liquid YCFA medium with higher inoculum (100 µL of 10⁻¹ or 10⁻² dilution)

2. **Primary Incubation**:
   - Place plates immediately in anaerobic jar or chamber
   - Incubate at 37°C for 3–14 days
   - Monitor daily for colony formation (some colonies may appear small and slow-growing)

3. **Colony Selection**:
   - After 3–7 days, select colonies with different morphologies
   - Mark interesting colonies for subculture
   - For liquid enrichments, streak onto fresh YCFA plates after 3–5 days

4. **Purification**:
   - Pick selected colonies and streak onto fresh YCFA plates
   - Incubate anaerobically at 37°C for 3–5 days
   - Repeat subculturing 2–3 times to ensure purity
   - Check purity by Gram staining and microscopy

5. **Storage**:
   - Maintain pure cultures in YCFA liquid medium (with 15% glycerol for long-term storage)
   - Freeze at -80°C in 15–20% glycerol
   - Alternatively, lyophilize pure cultures

#### Key Characteristics of Fusicatenibacter faecihominis Isolates

- **Morphology**: Gram-positive rods
- **Oxygen requirement**: Obligately anaerobic
- **Spore formation**: Non-spore-forming
- **Motility**: Non-motile
- **Growth characteristics**:
  - Optimal temperature: 37°C
  - Optimal pH: 6.8–7.0
  - Growth on YCFA medium: Visible colonies after 3–5 days
  - Colony appearance: Small, circular, convex, opaque colonies
- **Metabolic features**:
  - Saccharolytic (ferments carbohydrates)
  - Produces short-chain fatty acids (likely butyrate and/or propionate based on genus characteristics)

#### Strain Identification

The type strain H1-YC4 (DSM 113288, JCM 35911, also designated CLA-AA-H277) was identified through:

1. **16S rRNA gene sequencing**: Phylogenetic analysis revealed clustering within the genus *Fusicatenibacter*
2. **Whole-genome sequencing**: Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) and digital DNA-DNA hybridization (dDDH) with related species
3. **Phenotypic characterization**:
   - Gram staining and cell morphology
   - Carbohydrate fermentation profiles
   - Enzyme activities (API tests or similar)
   - Fatty acid methyl ester (FAME) analysis
4. **DNA G+C content**: Determined from genome sequence
5. **Polyphasic taxonomy**: Combined analysis of genotypic and phenotypic data

#### Additional Notes

- **Culturomics Approach**: This study employed multiple media types and conditions to maximize diversity of culturable bacteria. YCFA medium was one of several media used.
- **Enhanced Culturing**: The modified YCFA medium with additional nutrients and volatile fatty acids enhances growth of fastidious human gut anaerobes that are often difficult to culture.
- **Incubation Time**: Some strains may require extended incubation (up to 14 days) before visible growth appears.
- **Atmosphere**: Strict anaerobic conditions are critical throughout the isolation and cultivation process.
